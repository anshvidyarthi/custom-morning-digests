## ⚛️ Quantum & Deep Tech

**DARPA Quantum Benchmarking Initiative Advances Four Companies to Final Stage** — 
DARPA selected Atom Computing, Diraq, IBM and IonQ for Stage C of its Quantum Benchmarking Initiative, which seeks to determine whether any quantum computing approach can achieve utility-scale operation—meaning its computational value exceeds its cost—by 2033
. 
IonQ signed an agreement worth up to $300 million through 2029
, while 
Diraq will receive an initial $51 million funding tranche to support technical demonstrations and development of its fault-tolerant platform
. 
During the yearlong Stage B, QBI rigorously examined the organizations' detailed R&D plans, technical risks, mitigation strategies, and required prototypes
. [DARPA](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2026/four-more-teams-enter-quantum-benchmarking-initiative-final-stage)

**Oratomic Secures $775M at Unicorn Valuation for Fault-Tolerant Quantum** — 
Oratomic has raised a total of $775 million since its launch in March 2026 to accelerate the development of utility-scale, fault-tolerant quantum computers
. 
The company's funding round comes three months after its $300 million Series A; ARCH Venture Partners, Spark Capital, Khosla Ventures, Index Ventures, General Catalyst and Bezos Expeditions co-led the Series B round, bringing total funding to $775 million and raising valuation to $5.5 billion from $1.5 billion
. 
Bypassing intermediate Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) commercialization, Oratomic operates exclusively on a fault-tolerant technical roadmap utilizing reconfigurable neutral-atom arrays held in optical tweezers paired with quantum error correction protocols
. [Globe Newswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/08/3377341/0/en/oratomic-raises-775-million-in-total-funding-to-build-utility-scale-fault-tolerant-quantum-computers.html)

**IBM Nighthawk r2 Hits 25x Speed Boost with Sub-Microsecond Qubit Reset** — 
IBM's Nighthawk r2 quantum processor, with 120 programmable qubits and a groundbreaking high-speed qubit reset architecture, executes over 100,000 circuits per second—25x the circuit throughput of today's IBM Quantum Heron fleet—and has already demonstrated accurate observable estimation on circuits containing more than 7,500 gates, achieving a major 2026 milestone on the IBM Quantum Roadmap
. 
The active qubit reset leaves neighboring qubits undisturbed while reducing idle time between circuit runs to as little as a single microsecond, resulting in a dramatic increase in useful computation
. 
The system temporarily changes the qubit's effective T1 from about 200 microseconds to roughly 25 nanoseconds during the reset process, reducing the idle period between circuit runs to as little as one microsecond
. [IBM Quantum](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/nighthawk-r2)

**JEDEC Publishes First Industry Silicon Photonics Reliability Standard** — 
JEDEC Solid State Technology Association announced the release of JESD264: Silicon Photonics Qualification and Reliability Requirements, the first industry-wide standard designed to bring consistent qualification and reliability practices to silicon photonics devices, establishing a common baseline for testing and manufacturing controls to reduce deployment risk and support broader adoption in high-speed data-center, telecoms and AI networks
. 
The key features of JESD264 span four areas: comprehensive reliability protocols for SiPh dies and integrated chipsets; guidelines for component-level qualification, including modulators, photodiodes and waveguides; clear requirements for assembly processes and laser integration; and expectations for manufacturing process control and traceability
. 
Unlike mature CMOS technologies, silicon photonics has lacked standardized, industry-wide practices for qualification and reliability validation; electrical testing alone may miss optical-specific failures, including power degradation, insertion-loss changes, wavelength drift and coupling misalignment
. [Semiconductor Today](https://www.semiconductor-today.com/news_items/2026/oct/jedec-051026.shtml)

**Type-I Superconductor Breaks Time-Reversal Symmetry for First Time** — 
In a study published in Physical Review Letters, researchers report the discovery of a type I superconductor that breaks time-reversal symmetry, with evidence that YbSb₂ exhibits spontaneous internal magnetic fields when entering its superconducting state
. 
Researchers grew single crystals of YbSb₂, a material with an atomic layout found in both conventional and unconventional superconductors, and confirmed it was a type I superconductor by checking how it handled magnetic fields—unlike type II superconductors which let magnetic fields seep inside, type I materials push them out completely
. 
To see how YbSb₂ behaved, researchers cooled the crystals to near absolute zero and implanted muons directly into the material; as the material cooled into its superconducting state, spontaneous internal magnetic fields appeared
. [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-scientists-superconductor-reversal-symmetry.html)

**Vacuum Fluctuations Boost Superconductor Critical Temperature by 5.4%** — 
Researchers have demonstrated that quantum fluctuations in supposedly empty space can strengthen superconductivity, raising the transition temperature of an ultrathin material by as much as 5.4%, opening the possibility of using specially engineered vacuum environments to control quantum materials without direct contact or external driving
. 
Researchers observed that the critical temperature can increase by up to 5.4% in a six-layer NbSe2 device, while the critical current and critical magnetic field are significantly enhanced near the superconducting transition, representing the first experimental observation of vacuum-fluctuation-enhanced superconductivity
. [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260930225456.htm)

## Worth a deeper look

**DARPA's Stage C Verification Marks Inflection Point for Quantum Hardware** — The advancement of four companies to DARPA's final quantum benchmarking stage represents more than routine program progression—it signals that multiple hardware approaches have cleared rigorous government scrutiny of their paths to commercially viable fault tolerance by 2033. 
As an independent research and development arm of the U.S. Department of Defense, DARPA launched the QBI in 2024 to determine whether an industrially useful fault-tolerant quantum computer can be built by 2033, defining such a system as one whose computational value will exceed its cost; IBM has successfully progressed through the QBI's assessment process, including the first two stages that analyzed comprehensive R&D plans and risk mitigation approaches
. The diversity of selected modalities—
Universal Quantum's trapped-ion technology using charged atoms held in place by electric fields, and Oratomic's neutral atoms approach
—suggests DARPA sees multiple credible paths forward. 
Diraq announced it will deploy the world's first silicon spin quantum computer to operate in a shared commercial data centre with Equinix in Sydney, Australia before the end of 2026, following rapid U.S. growth with headquarters in Palo Alto, laboratories in Chicago and Albuquerque and a technology hub in Los Angeles, having also secured a Letter of Intent for $38M from the U.S. Department of Commerce under the CHIPS and Science Act
. The Stage C verification phase will test hardware against detailed benchmarks, providing the quantum industry's first independent, government-validated technical assessments at scale.
