## Worth a deeper look

**Revolution Medicines' daraxonrasib represents first major survival advance in pancreatic cancer**

The RASolute 302 trial results presented at ASCO on May 31, 2026, mark a watershed moment in pancreatic cancer treatment. Daraxonrasib, an oral RAS(ON) multi-selective inhibitor, achieved median overall survival of 13.2 months compared to 6.5 months with standard chemotherapy — nearly doubling survival in a disease where meaningful progress has been exceedingly rare. The trial met all primary and key secondary endpoints, demonstrating a 60% reduction in death risk while maintaining a manageable safety profile with low treatment discontinuation rates.

What makes daraxonrasib particularly notable is its mechanism. RAS proteins, mutated in approximately 95% of pancreatic cancers, have historically been considered "undruggable" targets. Daraxonrasib works by targeting the active, GTP-bound state of multiple RAS isoforms, including KRAS G12C, G12D, G12V, and G12R — the most common mutations driving pancreatic cancer. This multi-selective approach means the drug is effective across a broad patient population, not just those with specific mutations.

The FDA moved with remarkable speed on expanded access, issuing a "safe to proceed" letter just two days after receiving the application on April 28, 2026. This reflects both the unmet medical need in pancreatic cancer and the strength of the Phase 3 data. Patients with previously treated metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma now have access to daraxonrasib outside the clinical trial setting while regulatory submissions proceed.

For context, pancreatic cancer five-year survival rates have remained largely unchanged for decades, hovering around 12% for all stages combined and under 3% for metastatic disease. Standard second-line chemotherapy options have historically extended survival by only weeks to a few months. Daraxonrasib's median overall survival of 13.2 months — achieved in a second-line setting after patients had already progressed on standard therapy — represents the kind of advance that changes treatment paradigms. Revolution Medicines plans to submit the data to global regulatory authorities, with additional Phase 3 trials evaluating daraxonrasib in first-line metastatic and adjuvant settings already underway.
