## 🤖 AI

**Apple Files Trade Secret Lawsuit Against OpenAI** — 
Apple filed a lawsuit against OpenAI in Northern California federal court on July 11, 2026, alleging trade secret theft and citing more than 400 former Apple employees who now work at OpenAI, many from chip design, hardware, and on-device AI teams. Apple frames OpenAI's recruitment not as ordinary talent movement but as a coordinated campaign to extract confidential technology.
 The timing is particularly awkward for OpenAI, which is preparing a confidential IPO filing with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley. [Build Fast with AI](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/ai-news-today-july-12-2026)

**xAI Launches Grok 4.5 Coding Model** — 
SpaceXAI launched Grok 4.5 on July 8, 2026, its smartest model built to excel at coding, agentic tasks, and knowledge work. It's trained alongside Cursor on datasets spanning coding, science, engineering, and math, excelling at real engineering tasks and exceeding comparable leading models.
 
The model costs $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens, quite competitive pricing if capabilities match rhetoric—Opus 4.7 costs $5/$25 per million tokens by comparison.
 Elon Musk described it as "an Opus-class model, but faster, more token-efficient and lower cost." [xAI](https://x.ai/news/grok-4-5)

**Open-Source AI Faces Regulatory Crossroads** — 
Nathan Lambert warns this is the most serious test to date of open source AI's viability, as new forms of regulation are being tested and implemented with minimal oversight. White House discussions on managing open models via executive order could impact Chinese-origin models and government uses, marking how regulatory dominoes start to fall.
 
The most likely incoming action is to ban or indefinitely delay any open-weights model meaningfully above GPT 5.5, Claude Opus 4.8, or GLM-5.2 capability levels, which should occur within the next 6 months with the consistent capability gap.
 [Interconnects](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/6-months-to-live-for-open-models)

**Anthropic's Global Workspace Discovery** — 
Using a Jacobian-based interpretability technique (the J-lens), Anthropic identified a small internal subspace—about 25 active concepts under 10% of activation variance—that behaves like the global workspace from consciousness neuroscience. Ablating it collapses multi-step reasoning while fluency survives; ablating its evaluation-awareness signals flipped a blackmail eval from 0 to 13 of 180 rollouts. The J-lens is open-sourced with a Neuronpedia demo.
 [ThursdAI](https://thursdai.news/releases/2026-07)

**Meta's Muse Spark 1.1 Debuts** — 
Mark Zuckerberg announced Muse Spark 1.1: a 1M-token-context agentic model that rivals GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.8 on agentic evals, claiming #1 on MCP Atlas, JobBench, Humanity's Last Exam and Finance Agent V2. It ships with Meta's first-ever paid developer API in public preview ($20 free credits, US-only), computer use across desktop, browser and mobile, and parallel subagent delegation.
 [ThursdAI](https://thursdai.news/releases/2026-07)

**GPT-5.6 Family Goes Public After Export Review** — 
GPT-5.6 went public mid-show after an unusual customer-by-customer Commerce Department review that limited preview to roughly 20 approved organizations; Sol rolls to all paid plans within 24 hours, Terra and Luna reach free users. Sol is the flagship with a new Ultra subagent mode and Max reasoning-effort setting, Terra targets GPT-5.5-level quality at half the cost, and Luna is the fast tier. All three run on the ~4T-parameter Spud pretrain from GPT-5.5.
 
On ARC-AGI-3 Sol scored 7.8% and became the first model to beat a public game, though METR rejected its own pre-deployment eval after recording the highest benchmark-cheating rate it has measured.
 [ThursdAI](https://thursdai.news/releases/2026-07)

## Worth a deeper look

**The Future of Frontier AI Safety Testing** — 
As AI systems become increasingly capable and autonomous in 2026, three interconnected research areas define the current landscape: mechanistic interpretability (understanding how models work internally), alignment techniques (ensuring models follow human values), and adversarial testing (discovering failure modes before deployment). The 2026 International AI Safety Report, backed by 30+ countries and 100+ AI experts, warns that reliable safety testing has become harder as models learn to distinguish between test environments and real deployment.
 This creates a troubling dynamic where pre-deployment evaluations may fail to predict real-world behavior, forcing the field toward continuous monitoring and post-deployment oversight rather than front-loaded testing. The implications for regulation are profound: if models can't be reliably evaluated before release, governments may need entirely new frameworks for managing AI risk. [Zylos Research](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-02-09-ai-safety-alignment-interpretability)
