**Micron delivers record revenue as AI memory demand soars** — 
Micron posted blockbuster Q4 earnings with revenue quadrupling last quarter, reporting adjusted earnings of $33.42 per share, beating the LSEG consensus estimate of $31.61 per share, while revenue came in at $54.23 billion, above the expected $51.07 billion
. 
The results appeared to boost other chipmakers and AI-related companies more than Micron itself, whose shares are up 270% this year, with Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom all higher in early trading

**August PCE inflation meets revised bar but remains elevated** — 
The Commerce Department reported that the personal consumption expenditures (PCE) index rose 0.3% on a monthly basis in August and was up 3.4% from a year ago, with both figures cooler than the expectations of economists polled by LSEG, who predicted 0.4% and 3.7%, respectively
. 
Core PCE, which excludes volatile measurements of food and energy prices, was up 0.2% on a monthly basis and 3% year over year, both figures cooler than economists' expectations of 0.3% and 3.3%, respectively
. However, 
the Bureau of Economic Analysis made three technical changes to how it measures prices for portfolio management and investment advice fees, computer software, and legal services, with economists at RBC estimating the changes would trim core inflation by roughly 0.18 percentage points, and the result is visible in revised past readings: July's headline rate, originally reported at 3.7%, now reads 3.4%
. [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/august-2026-pce-inflation)

A global selloff in fixed-income has driven benchmark 10-year Treasury yields to the highest since 2007, and more than half of 173 respondents to a Markets Pulse survey last week predicted US 30-year yields will hit 6% by the end of the year

**Bond market revolt overshadows cooler inflation data** — The Treasury market's continued selloff despite Wednesday's softer-than-expected PCE inflation data signals a deeper shift in investor sentiment. 
The yield on the US 10-year Treasury note held around 5.23% on Wednesday, remaining near its highest level since 2007, while the 30-year yield climbed as high as 5.62%, reaching levels not seen since 2002, with bond yields remaining elevated amid persistent energy-driven inflation, a resilient US economy and hawkish signals from Federal Reserve officials that strengthened expectations for further rate hikes
. The disconnect between inflation data and yields reflects mounting concern about fiscal sustainability and the Fed's willingness to keep tightening even as methodological changes to the PCE calculation made historical inflation appear lower. 
The PCE inflation report helped shift expectations around the central bank's next policy meeting in October, with the CME FedWatch tool now showing a 65.1% chance the Fed holds rates steady, with a 34.9% chance of a further 25-basis-point hike
. The housing market is already feeling the pain, with 
the markets' momentum flagging as AI-driven tech stocks power through uncertainty, while the rest of the market remains encumbered by persistently high Treasury yields and oil prices
.
