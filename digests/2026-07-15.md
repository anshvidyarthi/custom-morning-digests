## 🧬 Biotech / Longevity / Neurotech

**Biogen presents Phase 2 tau data for diranersen, plans Phase 3 despite mixed results** — 
On July 14, 2026, Biogen presented Phase 2 CELIA data for diranersen, its tau-targeting Alzheimer's drug, at AAIC 2026 and announced plans to move into Phase 3, though the study missed its formal primary endpoint.
 
The data demonstrate a combination of meaningful clinical efficacy and robust biomarker effects, providing Phase 2 proof of concept for diranersen's tau-directed mechanism of action.
 
The presentation was easily the hottest ticket at AAIC, with experts saying "This is the first signal that we might be able to move beyond amyloid."
 [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/aaic-2026-biogen-delivers-hope-validation-with-anti-tau-alzheimers-win)

**Spero Therapeutics inks $1.1B licensing deal for third-generation anti-CD40L antibody** — 
Spero Therapeutics and Innovent Biologics announced an exclusive license agreement on July 14, 2026, for SP001 (IBI355), a third-generation Fc-silent anti-CD40L antibody, with Spero receiving exclusive rights worldwide excluding Greater China.
 
Spero expects to advance SP001 into a Phase 2 study in IgG4-RD patients in Q2 2027.
 
IBI355 is designed to avoid the Fc-mediated platelet activation that halted earlier anti-CD40L antibodies such as ruplizumab and toralizumab.
 [Globe Newswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/14/3326657/0/en/spero-therapeutics-and-innovent-biologics-announce-exclusive-license-for-sp001-ibi355-a-phase-2-ready-third-generation-anti-cd40l-antibody.html)

**Agenus discontinues Phase 3 metastatic trial, secures $340M to pursue neoadjuvant colon cancer strategy** — 
Agenus is halting financial support for the Phase 3 BATTMAN trial in late-line metastatic MSS colorectal cancer to prioritize neoadjuvant development of the same regimen.
 
The company announced on July 13, 2026, a private placement of approximately $85 million in upfront gross proceeds, with up to an additional $255 million upon full exercise of purchase warrants, which is expected to fund completion of ROBBIN with runway through year-end 2031.
 
Phase 2 data from NEST and UNICORN trials showed pathologic complete response rates of approximately 30% and major pathologic response rates of 35-40%.
 [CancerNetwork](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/agenus-discontinues-phase-3-battman-trial-metastatic-mss-colorectal-cancer)

**Q32 Bio's alopecia drug bempikibart hits Phase 2a endpoint, stock soars 80%** — 
On July 13, 2026, Q32 Bio reported positive 36-week topline data from Part B of its SIGNAL-AA Phase 2a trial testing bempikibart in 33 patients with severe or very severe alopecia areata, showing a mean 35.3% reduction in SALT score in the mITT population, with 40.0% achieving SALT-20 and 44.0% reaching SALT30 and SALT50 at Week 36.
 
Bempikibart was generally well tolerated with no treatment-related serious or Grade 3+ adverse events.
 
Q32 intends to advance a registration-directed program in the first half of 2027.
 [Q32 Bio](https://ir.q32bio.com/news-releases/news-release-details/q32-bio-announces-positive-36-week-topline-results-part-b-signal)

**InnoCare's TYK2 inhibitor soficitinib succeeds in Phase 3 atopic dermatitis trial** — 
InnoCare announced on July 15, 2026, that registrational Phase 3 clinical study results of novel TYK2 inhibitor soficitinib (ICP-332) met the primary endpoint in patients with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis, demonstrating statistical significance and clinically meaningful improvement.
 
The Phase 3 study is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter registrational clinical trial.
 [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/innocare-announces-phase-iii-study-results-of-tyk2-inhibitor-soficitinib-meet-primary-endpoint-in-patients-with-atomic-dermatitis)

**FDA expands CRISPR therapy Casgevy to children as young as 2 years old** — 
The U.S. FDA cleared the world's first CRISPR-based gene therapy for use in children as young as two years old on July 1, 2026, an expansion that places a potentially curative treatment within reach of approximately 5,500 additional American children before years of accumulated organ damage can narrow their options.
 
The therapy uses CRISPR/Cas9 to introduce a targeted double-strand break in the erythroid-specific enhancer region of the BCL11A gene, disrupting BCL11A's ability to repress gamma-globin promoters.
 [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319696/20260704/crispr-sickle-cell-therapy-now-cleared-children-starting-age-two.htm)

**Drug Farm Biotechnology closes $55M Series D for ROSAH syndrome rare disease drug** — 
Drug Farm Biotechnology Co. Ltd. closed a $55 million Series D round to advance its AI-developed alpha-protein kinase 1 (ALPK1) inhibitor, DF-003, in a Phase 3 trial for ROSAH syndrome, a rare genetic disease that can lead to blindness.
 The financing signals continued investor appetite for AI-driven rare disease drug discovery despite broader biotech market headwinds. [BioWorld](https://www.bioworld.com/topics/84-bioworld)

**Akero team launches Avere Therapeutics with Chinese immunology drug** — 
The former executive team of Akero Therapeutics, recently sold to Novo Nordisk, has partnered with Fairmount to launch a publicly traded biotech centered around a long-acting immunology drug plucked from a Chinese firm, called Avere Therapeutics, led by Andrew Cheng, Kitty Yale, and William White.
 
They will work to develop an oral IL-23 targeted drug for psoriasis, ulcerative colitis, and other immunological conditions.

## Worth a deeper look

**Biogen's tau data marks inflection point for Alzheimer's research** — 
The connection between amyloid and tau is becoming clearer, experts told BioSpace, with amyloid indicating early signs of Alzheimer's while tau accompanies cognitive decline, and Biogen's presentation on diranersen was easily the hottest ticket at AAIC 2026.
 
The company has committed to a Phase 3 trial based on biomarker and clinical signals observed in a mid-stage study that missed the dementia-related primary endpoint.
 
With two disease-modifying anti-amyloid therapies now available, tau presents an important opportunity for expanding Alzheimer's treatment options, coming as the Alzheimer's pipeline continues to grow and evolve, with 75% of current trials now aimed at pathways beyond amyloid and tau.
 The decision to advance despite a formal primary miss reflects how much unmet need remains in neurodegenerative disease—and how biomarker signals are increasingly shaping regulatory and commercial strategy in CNS drug development.
