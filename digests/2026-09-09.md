## 🧬 Biotech / Longevity / Neurotech


**Revolution Medicines wins FDA approval for first broad RAS-targeted pancreatic cancer drug** — The FDA approved Rasonque (daraxonrasib) on August 26 for metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma following at least one prior therapy, based on Phase 3 data showing median survival of 13.2 months versus 6.7 months on chemotherapy. The once-daily oral RAS inhibitor represents the first approved therapy from a new class of RAS(ON) multi-selective inhibitors and addresses one of oncology's highest unmet needs.
 [Revolution Medicines](https://ir.revmed.com/news-releases/news-release-details/us-fda-approves-revolution-medicines-rasonquetm-daraxonrasib)


**Novartis hit with third trial setback in one week after del-desiran fails** — Novartis shares fell 12.4% premarket on September 8 after del-desiran failed its Phase 3 HARBOR study in myotonic dystrophy type 1, showing no significant improvement over placebo on hand opening time. The failure follows pelacarsen's cardiovascular trial miss days earlier and pauses of eight autoimmune CAR-T trials after three patient deaths.
 
Del-desiran was the centerpiece of Novartis's $12 billion Avidity Biosciences acquisition and had been touted as a potential blockbuster, raising questions about the drugmaker's M&A-driven growth strategy.
 [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/novartis-stock-falls-12-third-131040046.html)


**AbbVie's etentamig meets Phase 3 endpoints in relapsed multiple myeloma** — The CERVINO trial showed etentamig achieved a 74% objective response rate versus 45.7% for standard therapies (p<0.0001) in triple-class exposed relapsed/refractory myeloma patients, with a 60% reduction in disease progression risk (HR=0.40). The BCMA×CD3 bispecific T-cell engager demonstrated low rates of cytokine release syndrome with monthly dosing following a single step-up dose.
 
Full results will be presented at the International Myeloma Society meeting September 23–26 in Glasgow.
 [AbbVie](https://news.abbvie.com/2026-09-03-AbbVie-Announces-Positive-Topline-Results-from-the-Phase-3-CERVINO-Trial-Showing-Etentamig-Significantly-Improved-Response-Rate-and-Progression-Free-Survival-in-Patients-with-Relapsed-Refractory-Multiple-Myeloma)


**Roche licenses trispecific antibody from Simcere in $1.53 billion deal** — Roche acquired exclusive global rights to SIM0660, a tri-specific antibody targeting CD79a, CD19, and CD3, with potential across B-cell-mediated diseases. Simcere Zaiming will receive $75 million upfront plus up to $1.455 billion in milestones and tiered double-digit royalties.
 
The molecule combines a CD3-engaging arm with dual B-cell antigen targeting to induce T cell-mediated cytotoxicity while limiting cytokine release, positioning it as a potential first-in-class approach.
 [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/simcere-zaiming-enters-exclusive-license-agreement-with-roche-for-sim0660-global-development)


**In vivo CAR-T gene therapy shows promise in multiple sclerosis trial** — A small clinical trial published in The New England Journal of Medicine demonstrated that a modified virus delivering genetic instructions directly into the bloodstream successfully prompted the body to produce CAR-T cells that eliminate disease-causing B cells. The approach, which engineers immune cells inside patients rather than ex vivo, showed significant functional improvements with manageable side effects.
 
The method promises to be cheaper and faster than current CAR-T therapies, though researchers stress the need for long-term monitoring of potential risks.
 [Quicknews](https://www.quicknews.co.za/2026/09/07/in-vivo-car-t-cell-therapy-alleviates-multiple-sclerosis/)


**Cleveland Clinic CRISPR cholesterol therapy maintains one-year benefit** — A first-in-human Phase 1 trial showed that a single infusion of CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing therapy reduced LDL cholesterol by 52.5% and triglycerides by 47.8% at 12 months in patients treated with the highest dose who had medication-resistant lipid disorders. The 15-patient trial, which aimed to confirm durability of reductions seen at two months, reported no serious adverse events related to therapy.
 
Results were presented at the 2026 European Society of Cardiology meeting and published simultaneously in the New England Journal of Medicine.
 [Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-09-crispr-gene-therapy-safely-lowers.html)


**Gene-edited pig kidney bridges patient to human transplant in landmark first** — A 66-year-old patient with end-stage kidney disease remained dialysis-free for nine months after receiving a pig kidney xenotransplant in January 2025, then became the first to successfully transition to a human kidney transplant. The eGenesis porcine organ carried 69 genetic modifications using CRISPR/Cas9, including virus inactivation, removal of rejection-triggering genes, and insertion of human compatibility genes.
 
The case was detailed by Massachusetts General Hospital researchers in The Lancet.
 [Progress](https://www.progress.org.uk/pig-kidney-serves-as-bridge-to-human-transplant-in-first-reported-case/)


**FDA approves Rasonque at $39,800 per month as oncologists hail breakthrough** — Revolution Medicines set the wholesale acquisition cost at $39,800 for a 30-day supply, totaling $477,600 annually before discounts. The approval, which came just over one month after submission thanks to a priority review voucher, drew standing ovations when Phase 3 data were presented at ASCO in May, with patients not only living longer but showing improved quality of life metrics.
 [Lambda Bio](https://afs.lambda-bio.com/blog/top-biotech-news-08-september-2026/)

## Worth a deeper look

**The Novartis pipeline crisis deepens** — 
The failure of del-desiran in myotonic dystrophy on September 8 marks Novartis's third clinical setback in seven days, following pelacarsen's cardiovascular trial miss and safety-driven pauses of eight CAR-T studies. Del-desiran was the lead asset from the company's $12 billion Avidity Biosciences acquisition last year and had been positioned by CEO Vas Narasimhan as a potential blockbuster with peak sales estimates of $3.1 billion.
 
Analysts at Barclays noted that del-desiran and pelacarsen together represented roughly $5 billion in risk-adjusted peak sales, but more critically, the del-desiran failure casts doubt on the Avidity acquisition rationale and Novartis's ability to offset patent expiries through M&A.
 
The stock dropped 9%, wiping approximately $29.6 billion off market value, and the setbacks increase pressure on Narasimhan's strategy to rebuild the pipeline through dealmaking as the company faces declining sales of older drugs like Entresto.
 Despite positive remibrutinib data in MS last week, the concentration of failures raises fundamental questions about Novartis's clinical development capabilities and due diligence processes.
