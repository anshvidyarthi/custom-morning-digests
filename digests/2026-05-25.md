## 🧬 Biotech / Longevity / Neurotech

**Eli Lilly's retatrutide achieves 28.3% weight loss in Phase 3 obesity trial** — 

Eli Lilly announced positive Phase 3 results for retatrutide, its investigational triple agonist (GIP, GLP-1, glucagon), with patients receiving the highest 12 mg weekly dose losing an average of 28.3% of body weight over 80 weeks, and more than 45% of participants losing at least 30% of their weight—results comparable to bariatric surgery outcomes.
 
A pre-specified blinded extension showed durability and further reduction to 30.3% body weight loss by week 104 among those initially assigned to 12 mg.
 
Cardiometabolic improvements accompanied weight loss, including a 24.1 cm waist reduction and favorable changes in non-HDL cholesterol, triglycerides, systolic blood pressure, and hs-CRP.

## Worth a deeper look

**Eli Lilly's retatrutide sets new weight-loss benchmark in first-line obesity competition**

The May 21 Phase 3 TRIUMPH-1 results for retatrutide mark a notable escalation in the GLP-1 obesity market, where Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk are locked in competition for next-generation therapies. Retatrutide is a first-in-class triple agonist targeting GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptors—one more pathway than tirzepatide (Zepbound), which targets GIP and GLP-1. The 28.3% average weight loss at 80 weeks in non-diabetic patients represents a new high-water mark for pharmacologic obesity treatment, with the extension cohort reaching 30.3% at 104 weeks. Analysts immediately flagged that these results match or exceed the typical 25-30% weight loss seen after bariatric surgery, the current gold standard for severe obesity.

The trial enrolled 2,339 participants across all dose arms, and efficacy held across the tested range: 4 mg, 9 mg, and 12 mg doses all met primary and key secondary endpoints. The 12 mg arm saw 65.3% of patients reach a BMI below 30 kg/m², and 45.3% achieved at least 30% weight loss—outcomes that position retatrutide as a potential first-line therapy if approved. Importantly, the cardiometabolic benefits tracked with weight reduction: a 24.1 cm waist circumference decrease, improvements in non-HDL cholesterol and triglycerides, and reductions in systolic blood pressure and hs-CRP. These markers suggest that the drug could deliver cardiovascular risk reduction beyond weight loss alone, a key consideration for payers and guideline committees.

The safety profile was consistent with incretin-based therapies, dominated by GI side effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea). Discontinuation rates increased with dose—11.3% at 12 mg versus 4.9% on placebo—but remained within the range seen for approved GLP-1 agents. Lilly plans to present detailed data at the American Diabetes Association Scientific Sessions in June and expects Phase 3 results from TRIUMPH-2 (type 2 diabetes) and TRIUMPH-3 (established cardiovascular disease) later this year. If those trials are positive, retatrutide could file for approval in multiple indications by early 2027.

For Lilly, the strategic importance is clear: retatrutide extends the company's obesity franchise beyond tirzepatide and orforglipron (the oral GLP-1 approved in April 2026 as Foundayo). It also narrows Novo Nordisk's window to maintain leadership in the category. Novo's CagriSema (cagrilintide/semaglutide dual agonist) is still in Phase 3, and while oral semaglutide launched in January 2026 (Wegovy oral), its weight-loss efficacy lags injectable formulations. The question for Lilly is how to position three overlapping obesity assets—tirzepatide (approved), orforglipron (oral, recently approved), and retatrutide (pending)—without cannibalizing sales. The most likely strategy: tirzepatide for patients prioritizing convenience (weekly injection), orforglipron for needle-averse patients willing to accept slightly lower efficacy, and retatrutide for patients with severe obesity or those who need maximal weight reduction.

The broader implication for the obesity market is that the efficacy ceiling continues to rise, and patient expectations with it. A 15% weight loss, once considered excellent for a pharmaceutical, is now table stakes. The new battleground is 25-30% reduction with tolerable side effects and durable maintenance. Retatrutide, if approved, will set that benchmark.

**Immix Biopharma's NXC-201: a 95% complete response rate in heavily pre-treated AL amyloidosis signals CAR-T's rare disease potential**

Immix Biopharma's May 21 interim update from the NEXICART-2 trial is one of the most striking CAR-T datasets of 2026, not because of the mechanism—BCMA-targeted CAR-T is well-validated in multiple myeloma—but because of the disease (AL amyloidosis) and the response depth. AL amyloidosis is a plasma cell disorder where clonal plasma cells produce misfolded light chains that deposit in organs (heart, kidneys, liver), causing progressive organ failure and death. It's rare (roughly 4,000-5,000 new U.S. cases annually) and notoriously difficult to treat, with patients cycling through chemotherapy regimens and often relapsing.

NXC-201 is a sterically-optimized BCMA CAR-T with a proprietary design intended to reduce off-target activation. The NEXICART-2 trial enrolled heavily pretreated patients—median of four prior lines of therapy—and treated them with NXC-201 as a fifth-line option. The updated data shows that all four patients who were MRD-negative at the December 2025 ASH presentation have now converted to complete response (CR), bringing the trial's CR rate to 95% (19 of 20 patients). All CRs were reached within one year of dosing, and no relapses have been observed to date. All subsequently enrolled patients for whom MRD results are available are MRD-negative at one month.

The clinical significance is that MRD-negativity in AL amyloidosis is a strong predictor of organ response and survival. Achieving it with a single infusion in patients who have exhausted multiple lines of therapy is unprecedented. For context, standard therapies (daratumumab, bortezomib, cyclophosphamide/dexamethasone combinations) rarely achieve durable CR in this population. The company has positioned NXC-201 as a potential "one-and-done" therapy, contrasting it with the typical two-year treatment course for AL amyloidosis.

Immix received FDA Breakthrough Therapy Designation for NXC-201 in January 2026, which accelerates regulatory timelines and signals FDA agreement that the drug addresses an unmet need. The company expects to present updated NEXICART-2 data in September 2026 and one-year follow-up data by end of March 2027, with a Biologics License Application (BLA) submission and commercial launch targeted for late 2027. The addressable market is modest but growing—Immix estimates roughly 38,500 relapsed/refractory AL amyloidosis patients in the U.S. in 2026, growing at 12% annually. At typical CAR-T pricing ($400K-$500K per patient), that's a $1.4-$2.8 billion annual sales opportunity if NXC-201 becomes standard of care.

The broader strategic read is that BCMA CAR-T, initially developed for multiple myeloma, is proving applicable across plasma cell-driven diseases. Immix's success in AL amyloidosis opens the door to other antibody-mediated disorders (systemic sclerosis, pemphigus, certain autoimmune diseases) where depleting plasma cells could deliver durable benefit. For investors and strategists watching the CAR-T space, NXC-201 is a proof point that the platform's value extends well beyond oncology.
