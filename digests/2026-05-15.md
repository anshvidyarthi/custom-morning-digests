## 🧬 Biotech / Longevity / Neurotech

**Beacon Therapeutics' gene therapy shows sustained 12-month benefit in rare eye disease** — 
Beacon Therapeutics reported at ARVO 2026 that laruparetigene zovaparvovec (laru-zova) showed sustained improvements over 12 months in Phase II DAWN trial patients with X-linked retinitis pigmentosa (XLRP), with benefits in low luminance visual acuity and macular sensitivity, in line with previous nine-month data
. 
The gene therapy delivers a functional copy of the RPGRORF15 gene to restore rod and cone function in XLRP, and is also being evaluated in the pivotal VISTA trial with readout expected in H2 2026
. [Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/beacon-therapeutics-gene-therapy-shows-12-month-benefit/)

**Gene therapy clinical trials worldwide reach 3,900 as field accelerates** — 
An international team reported in the Journal of Gene Medicine that as of March 2023, 3,900 gene therapy clinical trials have been completed, are ongoing, or have been approved worldwide across 46 countries, with a searchable database consolidating information from trial databases, official agencies, and literature
. 
Most trials focus on cancer (68.3%) followed by inherited monogenic diseases (13.1%), with the US leading at 2,054 trials (52.7%), China at 651 (16.7%), and multicountry trials increasing from 130 in 2017 to 301 currently
. [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/an-updated-snapshot-of-gene-therapy-clinical-trials-worldwide)

**Nature reviews next-generation senolytics: from broad drugs to precision PROTACs** — 
A comprehensive Nature Aging review published in March 2026 describes how cellular senescence drives aging through SASP-mediated inflammation, while first-generation senolytics like navitoclax and dasatinib-quercetin exhibited dose-dependent thrombocytopenia and variable efficacy, prompting development of second-generation approaches including PROTAC-based senolytic degraders with improved selectivity
. The review emphasizes precision senotherapeutics tailored to specific tissue contexts and senescent cell subtypes. [Nature npj Aging](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41514-026-00355-z)

## Worth a Deeper Look

**Gene therapy clinical trials reach 3,900 worldwide—what the March 2026 update reveals**

The latest comprehensive analysis from the Journal of Gene Medicine's Gene Therapy Clinical Trials Worldwide database provides a crucial snapshot of a field accelerating toward clinical maturity. As of March 2023 (with the analysis published and widely discussed through early 2026), 3,900 gene therapy trials have been initiated, are ongoing, or have been approved across 46 countries—an unprecedented surge from previous years.

The geographical distribution tells a story of concentrated expertise and investment: the United States accounts for 52.7% of all trials (2,054), followed by China at 16.7% (651 trials), the UK at 6.3% (245), Germany at 2.9% (115), France at 1.7% (66), and Japan at 1.4% (55). But perhaps most significant is the growth in multicountry trials—from 130 in 2017 to 301 now—reflecting both increased international collaboration and the necessity of accessing patient populations across borders, particularly for rare and ultrarare diseases.

The disease focus remains heavily weighted toward cancer (68.3% of trials), with inherited monogenic diseases second at 13.1%. This distribution makes sense given cancer's prevalence and the relatively mature state of CAR-T and other oncology gene therapies, but the growing pipeline in rare genetic diseases signals where the field's transformative potential may be most profound. For conditions like spinal muscular atrophy, hemophilia, inherited retinal diseases, and now XLRP, gene therapy is moving from "promising experimental approach" to "standard of care consideration."

The acceleration is particularly notable since 2017, when the FDA approved voretigene neparvovec-rzyl (Luxturna) for inherited retinal disease—the first AAV-based gene therapy in the US. That approval opened regulatory pathways and validated the AAV platform, leading to a cascade of clinical development. The field has also seen the implementation of gene editing technologies including CRISPR/Cas9, base editing, and prime editing in human trials, with Casgevy (exagamglogene autotemcel) becoming the first CRISPR/Cas9-based therapy approved by the FDA in late 2023 for sickle cell disease.

Challenges remain substantial: manufacturing scalability, immunogenicity, durability of effect, cost ($2-3.5 million per patient for approved therapies), and the ongoing question of how healthcare systems will integrate these one-time treatments into payment models designed for chronic care. But the data make clear that gene therapy has crossed from "experimental" to "operational" status, with clinical trial activity now distributed globally and targeting an expanding range of indications beyond cancer.

The March 2026 update represents not just a data milestone but a field reaching critical mass—where learnings from thousands of trials begin to consolidate into best practices, where manufacturing capacity scales to meet demand, and where regulatory pathways become well-defined enough to support efficient translation from preclinical proof-of-concept to approved therapy.

[AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/an-updated-snapshot-of-gene-therapy-clinical-trials-worldwide) | [Journal of Gene Medicine](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jgm.3721)
