## 📈 Finance & Stock Markets


**Markets extend losses as oil and rates pressure equities** — The S&P 500 slipped 0.24%, the Dow lost 0.58%, and the Nasdaq fell 0.35% on September 9.
 
Broader selling pressure hit all major indices throughout the session, with the Nasdaq leading declines and the VIX surging 5.47% as the 10-year bond yield climbed.
 
Energy was the standout sector performer on September 10 with XLE jumping 0.67%, while utilities and materials also gained as investors sought safety in defensive areas.
 [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-sept-09-2026)


**10-year Treasury yield climbs to 4.85%, highest since October 2023** — The yield on the 10-year US Treasury note rose toward 4.85% on Wednesday after the Treasury Department tripled its buyback program to $6 billion.
 
Mounting fiscal concerns, record corporate debt issuance with AI companies accounting for more than $1.5 trillion in new borrowing, and continued weakness in the Japanese yen as Tokyo repeatedly sold Treasuries to support its currency added upward pressure on yields.
 
Yields in the long-end have surged this year as high oil prices due to the Iran war raised underlying inflation risks, magnified by record-setting bond supply and Japanese yen pressure driving Tokyo to repeatedly sell Treasury securities.
 [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/government-bond-yield)


**Brent crude surges above $101 as US-Iran conflict escalates** — Brent rose to $101.25 per barrel on September 10, up 13.88% over the past month and 52.56% compared to the same time last year, driven by escalating US-Iran tensions.
 
The US military destroyed five Iranian tankers carrying crude following attempts to strike a US Navy warship with ballistic missiles, while Iran claimed to have attacked two American vessels and eight oil tankers in the Gulf.
 
The escalation heightened concerns over disruption to global oil supplies around the Strait of Hormuz, as Iran-backed Houthi militants also targeted energy infrastructure in southern Saudi Arabia, including the 400,000-barrel-a-day Jazan refinery.
 [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil)


**PPI inflation rises 0.4% in August, annual rate at 5.4%** — The producer price index increased a seasonally adjusted 0.4% for the month in line with consensus, with the annual rate reaching 5.4%, still well above the Fed's 2% inflation target and 0.1 percentage point higher than expected.
 
Over three-fourths of the broad-based rise in final demand goods can be attributed to prices for final demand energy, which moved up 4.2%, with over a third traced to prices for diesel fuel which jumped 24.1%.
 The report comes one day before Friday's August CPI data, the final inflation update before the Fed's September 15-16 meeting. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/10/ppi-inflation-report-august-2026.html)


**Fed rate-hike odds climb to 63% ahead of September 16 FOMC decision** — Interest rate markets show a 63% probability of a 25 basis point hike at the next FOMC meeting in seven days, with the implied move pricing in roughly +15.7 basis points to the fed funds midpoint.
 
By early September, markets shifted to roughly a two-thirds probability of a 25 basis point rate hike, driven by a strong August jobs report and persistent inflation concerns reflected in rising prices for the hike outcome.
 
The next Federal Reserve decision is on Wednesday September 16, 2026 at 2:00 PM US Eastern Time with a press conference at 2:30 PM ET, carrying updated economic projections.
 [Rate Probability](https://rateprobability.com/fed)

Macy's is nearing the end of a three-year turnaround plan under CEO Tony Spring that aims to spark growth and invest in locations that perform well against a challenging backdrop for department stores.
 The company is one of several retailers being watched for signals about consumer health ahead of the critical holiday season. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/09/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)


**Apple unveils foldable iPhone Duo at $2,000 but stock closes flat** — Apple unveiled its first foldable phone, the iPhone Duo, marking the biggest change to the iPhone in its nearly 20-year history, alongside the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max, AirPods 5, Apple Watch Series 12 and Apple Watch Ultra 4.

Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives told CNBC the iPhone Duo is the first new Apple product in 10 years since the AirPods that could "move the needle," potentially kicking off a larger innovation cycle.

## Worth a deeper look

**Energy sector rotation continues as AI infrastructure demand collides with geopolitical risk** — 
Energy is the best-performing sector so far this year thanks in part to a roughly 12% spike in oil prices, with industrial, consumer defensive, and energy stocks leading the market higher in 2026 as technology names falter and investors look beyond the AI trade for returns, benefiting from tailwinds stemming from the AI data center buildout, cost-conscious consumer spending, and rising oil prices.
 
Energy and value-oriented sectors have quietly outperformed, benefiting from elevated oil prices and AI data center power demand, while market leadership is broadening into small caps, healthcare, financials, and industrials, rewarding diversification after years of narrow mega-cap dominance.
 
The index's health rests heavily on semiconductors—that concentration is the risk deserving respect—while energy, industrials, healthcare, and financials aren't rallying on nothing, benefiting from the AI power build-out, a steadier rate environment, and valuations that never got stretched.
 The shift marks one of the most dynamic sector environments in years, with money moving violently between corners of the market as passive investors inadvertently make concentrated bets on AI-adjacent chip names that now represent ~42% of the S&P 500 tech sector and ~20% of the full index.
