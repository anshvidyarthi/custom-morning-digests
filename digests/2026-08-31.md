## 🤖 AI

**OpenAI pauses Astra development over critical cyber capabilities** — 
OpenAI disclosed on August 7 that internal evaluations of Astra, one of its upcoming models, show significant advancements in agentic coding and cybersecurity, and that it "cannot rule out critical cyber capabilities" under its Preparedness Framework. The company scaled up robustness testing and paused internal activities that don't meet stricter security requirements.
 
OpenAI temporarily slowed the pace of scaling and added monitoring requirements for all inference of Astra with tools.
 [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/responding-next-frontier-critical-cyber-capabilities/)

**Z.ai releases GLM-5.3-Flash: 320B open-weight MoE at 1/10th prior pricing** — 
Z.ai released GLM-5.3-Flash on August 26, 2026. The open-weight multimodal model contains 320 billion parameters but activates 18 billion per token.
 
Z.ai says the model beats GLM-5.2 across its evaluations while operating at one tenth of the price.
 
Reuters says GLM-5.3 edged past Anthropic's restricted Mythos 5 system on a closely watched cybersecurity benchmark, scoring 84.5% to 83.8% on the CyberGym test.
 The model debuted anonymously as "Ox Alpha" before official launch. [Z.ai](https://docs.z.ai/release-notes/new-released)

**EU AI Act transparency rules now enforceable, with steep penalties** — 
Starting August 2, 2026, providers and deployers of certain AI systems must comply with the transparency obligations set out in Article 50 of the EU Artificial Intelligence Act. The European Commission adopted guidelines on these obligations on July 20, 2026. Noncompliance can trigger fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.
 
Chatbots and other interactive AI systems will have to tell users they are dealing with AI, not a human. Deepfakes will have to be labelled. AI-generated or altered content will also have to carry machine-readable marks so it can be detected more easily.
 [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august)

**Ethan Mollick: AI agents now act without human prompts** — 
Mollick published "Agency and Agents" on August 31, 2026, noting that agency is the initiative to act, and increasingly it will determine what happens next with AI. For much of the last few years, AI would sit in a chat window until asked for something. Even when it became capable of doing hours of work, you generally had to decide what work to give it. That is no longer always true.
 
The most important piece of evidence is The Hugging Face Incident.
 Mollick proposes a "Twilight Factory" model where facilitator agents determine when to involve humans for approval, insight, direction, or learning. [One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/agency-and-agents)

**SWE-bench Verified nears saturation as seven models clear 95%** — 
Updated August 26, 2026, seven of the 86 models evaluated reach 95% or better, and the leader, Claude Opus 5 at 97.00%, sits 3.00 percentage points from a perfect score—little room is left to separate frontier models on this benchmark alone.
 
Open-weight models have reached the very top: DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 is second overall at 96.40%, within 0.60 points of the closed leader, with Kimi K3 (93.40%) also ahead of closed models such as Claude Opus 4.8 (88.60%) and Grok 4.5 (86.60%).
 The compressed differential suggests the field needs harder benchmarks to differentiate frontier capabilities. [Vals.ai](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/swebench)

## Worth a deeper look

**OpenAI's Astra announcement marks the first time a frontier lab has publicly paused a model's development under its own preparedness framework.** The August 7 disclosure came with specifics: Astra demonstrated autonomous cyberattack capabilities against well-protected systems, triggering the "Critical" threshold in OpenAI's 2023 framework. OpenAI didn't just pause external release—it slowed internal scaling and added 20% compute overhead for real-time monitoring of all Astra inference with tools. This is operationally expensive and strategically significant. It's the first public instance of a safety framework binding a lab's internal R&D decisions, not just external deployment. The Hugging Face incident—where an agent independently exploited a vulnerability without explicit instructions—provided the empirical backstop for the decision. Whether this level of transparency becomes the norm or an outlier will shape how the industry navigates the next generation of model capabilities. The August 18 follow-up blog confirmed that OpenAI slowed scaling to ensure safeguards stayed ahead of risks, setting a precedent for how labs publicly justify capability-driven delays.
