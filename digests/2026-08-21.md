## ⚛️ Quantum

**Cornell breaks superconducting qubit manufacturing barrier** — 
Cornell researchers developed a method using krypton gas to deposit tantalum-based superconducting qubits at 200°C instead of 400°C, making the process compatible with commercial semiconductor foundries while preventing tantalum-silicon intermixing
. 
Qubits fabricated with the krypton-sputtered films achieved internal quality factors up to 16.9 million
. The work, published in Nature Materials, addresses a critical manufacturability bottleneck for scaling quantum hardware. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/cornell-researchers-lower-tantalum-qubit-deposition-temperature-to-200c-using-krypton-gas-sputtering/)

**Israel launches national post-quantum security consortium** — 
Allot has been appointed founding member and Chair of Israel's Post-Quantum Communications Consortium, uniting NVIDIA, RAD Data Communications, Ceragon, Classiq, and six Israeli universities to develop quantum-safe cryptographic defenses
. 
The consortium will evaluate hybrid protocols combining post-quantum cryptography with quantum key distribution across optical, mobile, satellite, data center, and internet communications
. [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/08/19/allot-launches-post-quantum-communications-consortium/)

**Pasqal traps atoms on photonic chip** — 
Pasqal demonstrated a photonic integrated circuit generating four optical traps that captured individual atoms in a neutral-atom quantum processor, achieving atom lifetimes comparable to bulk-optics systems
. 
Developed with subsidiary Aeponyx, the platform replaces free-space optical tables with silicon nitride photonic chips, addressing a major scaling bottleneck
. 
Pasqal aims to scale toward systems with more than 10,000 atoms and 100 logical qubits
. [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/08/10/pasqal-photonic-chip-control-neutral-atom-quantum-computers/)

**Q-CTRL runs 100-qubit Quantum Fourier Transform on IBM** — 
Q-CTRL researchers executed a 100-qubit Quantum Fourier Transform on IBM Quantum computers, representing the largest experimental QFT on any quantum hardware to date by a factor of two
. 
The team measured 11.4% process fidelity at 50 qubits and 1.8% at 80 qubits, with the correct output state remaining distinguishable above noise up to 100 qubits
. 
The convolutional compilation strategy ran on IBM Heron hardware, keeping the correct frequency visible despite error accumulation
. [Q-CTRL](https://q-ctrl.com/blog/breaking-the-100-qubit-barrier-executing-the-quantum-fourier-transform-at-scale-on-ibm-hardware)

**D-Wave publishes dual-rail error correction breakthrough** — 
D-Wave published Nature research demonstrating a two-qubit entangling gate for its dual-rail erasure architecture, with simulations indicating the design could reduce logical error rates by a factor of 10 for each increment in error correction
. 
The research demonstrates approximately 99.9% fidelity during two-qubit operations with gate times of about 500 nanoseconds enabled by native hardware-level error detection
. The work targets reduced physical qubit overhead for fault-tolerant systems. [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/08/05/d-wave-hardware-breakthrough-quantum-error-correction-fault-tolerant-computing/)

**Quantinuum installs Helios inside Oracle data center** — 
Quantinuum and Oracle announced a multi-year partnership installing Quantinuum's Helios quantum computer inside a US Oracle Cloud Infrastructure AI data center, making it available to OCI customers as a managed cloud service alongside GPU and HPC capacity
. 
The companies framed the deal around hybrid quantum-AI workloads targeting drug discovery, materials science, financial modeling, and optimization
. 
Oracle plans to preview its OCI quantum service in the coming months
. [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/quantinuum-puts-a-98-qubit-helios-machine-inside-oracles-ai-data-centers/)

**Microsoft-Quantinuum quantum error correction achieves 11–800× error reduction** — 
A Nature study from Microsoft Quantum and Quantinuum found that quantum error-correction techniques reduced computational errors by factors ranging from 11-fold to 800-fold compared with calculations on physical qubits, using carbon code and tesseract code across experiments with up to 12 logical qubits
. 
The researchers said the results suggest current quantum processors can already benefit from fault tolerance, although further hardware, real-time decoding, and scalable error-correction advances are needed
. [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/06/13/microsoft-and-quantinuum-report-on-major-gains-in-quantum-error-correction/)

## Worth a deeper look

**Neutral-atom hardware scaling converges on integrated photonics** — Pasqal's demonstration of on-chip atom trapping marks a pivotal transition for the fastest-scaling quantum modality. 
The silicon nitride photonic integrated circuit replaces free-space optical tables with waveguides, generating four individual optical traps via a single chip and enabling up to 50× reduction in optical hardware footprint
. 
Testing showed atom lifetimes of approximately 27.5 seconds, matching Pasqal's existing bulk-optics systems
. Neutral-atom systems have already demonstrated 1,000+ physical qubits, but optical control infrastructure becomes untenable at 10,000+ atoms. 
The Aeponyx acquisition, completed less than 18 months prior, positions Pasqal to vertically integrate photonic control—a capability that will differentiate at scale
. This is the hardware innovation that keeps neutral atoms competitive against superconducting and trapped-ion architectures into the logical-qubit era.
