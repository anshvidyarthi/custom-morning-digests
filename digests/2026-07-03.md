## ⚛️ Quantum

**Department of War launches $200M quantum sensing initiative** — 
The U.S. Department of War's Defense Innovation Unit has launched "Project Farseer," a $200 million initiative to rapidly integrate advanced quantum sensing and timing hardware into military operations
. 
The program is divided into four Lines of Effort: magnetometers, gravimeters, portable tactical clocks, and component technologies, all requiring adherence to Sensor Open Systems Architecture principles
. The initiative follows Executive Order 14411 and aims to enhance intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance capabilities in GPS-denied and electromagnetically contested environments. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Pentagon sets 2031 deadline for quantum-safe cryptography across all military systems** — 
The Department of War released its Post-Quantum Cryptography Strategy to eliminate cryptographic vulnerabilities across all military systems by December 31, 2031, explicitly banning certain workarounds like Quantum Key Distribution and outlining a dual-track approach for high-assurance and commercial solutions
. The strategy represents the most comprehensive public-sector timeline for migrating to quantum-resistant cryptographic primitives, reflecting mounting concerns about harvest-now-decrypt-later attacks. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Berkeley Lab simulates particle physics on IBM quantum hardware** — 
A Berkeley Lab scientist used IBM quantum hardware accessed through ORNL's Quantum Computer User Program to simulate hadronization, using 104 qubits on IBM's 156-qubit Heron processor to study string breaking
. 
The results matched earlier classical supercomputer work and suggested that part of the gluon string may behave like a finite-temperature gas before separation
. The demonstration shows quantum computers beginning to tackle quantum chromodynamics problems that overwhelm classical systems due to exponential scaling challenges. [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/07/01/researchers-use-ibm-quantum-hardware-to-model-a-key-particle-physics-process/)

**Microsoft Majorana 2 claims 20-second qubit lifetime, physicist pushes back** — 
Microsoft reported that its Majorana 2 processor achieved topological qubit lifetimes exceeding 20 seconds, more than 1,000 times longer than earlier devices, after replacing aluminum with lead in its superconducting material stack and redesigning the semiconductor structure, more than doubling the topological gap
. However, 
University of St Andrews physicist Henry Legg argues in a Nature "Matters Arising" paper that the "topological qubit" might simply be noise, assailing Microsoft's claims that it has demonstrated the technology
. The dispute echoes Microsoft's 2022 retraction and underscores the difficulty of definitively proving Majorana zero modes exist in condensed-matter systems. [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/06/02/microsoft-reports-advances-in-majorana-2-following-debate-over-last-years-topological-claims/) / [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/top-quantum-computer-expert-claims-microsofts-topological-qubit-doesnt-hold-up/)

**Archer Materials partners with IonQ on sovereign Australian quantum deployment** — 
Archer Materials has partnered with IonQ in a US$1.5 million agreement to advance its quantum computing capabilities, focusing on a joint study for on-shore quantum computer deployment in Australia and enhancing Archer's Quantum Machine Learning fraud detection models using IonQ's high-fidelity trapped-ion hardware, allowing Archer to continue graphene-based chip development while immediately generating revenue
. The deal reflects growing sovereign quantum computing strategies among U.S. allies seeking to avoid supply-chain dependencies on hardware manufactured outside national borders. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Duke and IonQ achieve tripartite entanglement across three quantum network nodes** — 
Researchers from Duke University and IonQ successfully demonstrated distributed tripartite entanglement across a three-node quantum network using remote atomic qubits, achieved without local two-qubit gates or post-selection, and violated the Mermin inequality, closing the detection loophole and verifying quantum non-locality
. The demonstration establishes a framework for modular quantum computing by connecting distinct processing nodes via photonic interconnects, a key step toward distributed quantum architectures. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**QPerfect and BTQ develop first circuit-level quantum one-shot signature scheme** — 
QPerfect and BTQ Technologies have developed the first circuit-level One-Shot Signature scheme, bridging the gap between cryptographic theory and executable quantum circuits, utilizing a "local quantum cryptography" framework with dual-layered program obfuscation for security
. One-shot signatures offer unique security properties relevant to quantum authentication protocols, though practical deployment depends on fault-tolerant quantum processors. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Quantum Leap Acquisition Corp closes $200M SPAC for frontier tech mergers** — 
Quantum Leap Acquisition Corp successfully closed its $200 million IPO, issuing 20 million units at $10.00 each on the NYSE, and explicitly excludes businesses in China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, or Macau to protect Western technology interests, aiming to merge with or acquire high-growth deep-tech firms in AI, quantum computing, and blockchain
. The vehicle represents one of several blank-check companies targeting quantum mergers in a consolidating market. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Sandia validates Quantinuum's 98-qubit Helios trapped-ion system** — 
Sandia National Laboratories and Quantinuum validated Helios, a 98-qubit trapped-ion quantum computer, demonstrating high fidelity rates for single and two-qubit gates, with its QCCD architecture featuring a rotatable ion storage ring and real-time control software, and volumetric benchmarks confirm the system's capabilities and the extreme computational resources needed to classically simulate its operations
. Helios represents Quantinuum's next-generation hardware following its H2 system. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**QuEra updates roadmap with fault-tolerant Libra system targeting 2028** — 
QuEra Computing updated its quantum roadmap, planning to launch the fault-tolerant Libra system on Amazon Braket by 2028, aiming for megaquop performance with 256 logical qubits from over 10,000 physical qubits, followed by a next-generation gigaquop-class system with over 1,000 logical qubits, focusing on computational chemistry, high-energy physics, and condensed matter physics
. The neutral-atom hardware features parallel atom-shuttling connectivity and room-temperature operation, distinguishing it from cryogenic competitors. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**QuEra and Los Alamos unveil transversal STAR architecture with 250x speedup** — 
QuEra Computing and Los Alamos National Laboratory introduced the transversal STAR architecture, a co-designed quantum computing framework that significantly reduces qubit overhead and speeds up fault-tolerant quantum simulation, achieving a 250x speedup by eliminating the need for magic state distillation and discrete gate synthesis, and by integrating qLDPC codes, it further reduces physical qubit requirements to 1,500-3,000
. The architecture is specifically optimized for neutral-atom hardware and materials science applications. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

## Worth a deeper look

**PsiQuantum breaks ground on utility-scale facility in Queensland** — 
PsiQuantum began construction on a utility-scale quantum computing facility at Moreton Bay Central in Queensland, Australia, backed by a joint $940 million (AUD) investment, which will house tens of thousands of silicon-photonic chips and a massive industrial cryoplant for cooling, and is strategically positioned in a developing precinct set to host events for the Brisbane 2032 Olympics
. The facility represents a shift from laboratory-scale demonstrations to industrial infrastructure, with photonic chips manufactured at GlobalFoundries in New York and integrated on-site. PsiQuantum also opened a Test & Validation Lab at Griffith University featuring the Poseidon cryogenic system for simultaneously testing multiple Omega silicon photonic chipsets. The company is targeting a million-qubit, fault-tolerant system by 2027-2028, one of the most aggressive timelines in the industry, though the photonic approach remains relatively unproven at scale compared to superconducting and trapped-ion platforms. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)
