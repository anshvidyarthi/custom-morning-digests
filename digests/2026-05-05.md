## 🧬 Biotech / Longevity / Neurotech

**Cytokinetics' aficamten (Myqorzo) meets dual Phase 3 endpoints in non-obstructive HCM** — Today Cytokinetics reported positive topline results from ACACIA-HCM, the pivotal Phase 3 trial of aficamten in non-obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (nHCM). The trial met both co-primary endpoints with statistical significance: a 3.0-point improvement in KCCQ-CSS (quality of life, p=0.021) and a 0.67 mL/kg/min improvement in peak VO₂ (exercise capacity, p=0.003) versus placebo at Week 36. Secondary endpoints including NYHA functional class and NT-proBNP also improved. Non-obstructive HCM affects roughly 600,000 Americans — about twice as many as the obstructive form — and currently has no approved therapy targeting the underlying hypercontractility. One notable safety signal: LVEF <50% occurred in 10% of aficamten patients versus 1% on placebo, with two serious heart failure events associated with ejection fraction drop, which will be a focus of regulatory review. **Eli Lilly bets $2.25B on Profluent's AI-designed recombinases** — Announced April 28, the deal between Lilly and Bezos-backed Profluent (Berkeley, CA) centers on AI-designed site-specific recombinases: custom enzymes trained by Profluent's protein language models to target exact genomic locations and insert large DNA payloads. Standard CRISPR tools are limited to short edits (tens to hundreds of base pairs); recombinases can handle full-gene insertions on the kilobase scale, which is critical for conditions where the disease gene is simply too large for CRISPR to replace. The partnership is sparse on program count and disease focus but the milestone structure runs to $2.25B. Lilly, flush with obesity and diabetes revenue, has opened a new Boston genetic medicine center and has been acquiring gene-editing assets at an accelerating pace. **BioAge's BGE-102 NLRP3 inhibitor shows best-in-class Phase 1 inflammation data** — April 21 data from BioAge's Phase 1 SAD/MAD trial showed oral BGE-102 (60 mg and 120 mg once daily) achieving ≥85% median reductions in hsCRP in participants with obesity and elevated baseline inflammation, with 93% of participants reaching normalized levels (≤2 mg/L) at Day 14. Critically for CNS applications, CSF drug levels crossed the IC90 threshold for NLRP3 suppression — meaning the drug reaches the brain at pharmacologically relevant concentrations, opening potential paths in Alzheimer's, retinal disease, and atherosclerosis. NLRP3 is an inflammasome complex increasingly implicated in aging-related inflammation ("inflammaging"), making a brain-penetrant, orally available inhibitor with a clean Phase 1 profile notable in the longevity pipeline. BioAge is hosting an R&D Day on May 8 to walk through the full data package and announce Phase 2 plans in cardiovascular disease. ## 🤖 AI

**Google, Microsoft, and xAI agree to pre-release NIST model evaluations** — NIST's Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) announced agreements with Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and xAI today, joining OpenAI and Anthropic (both participating since 2024) in giving the US Commerce Department early access to frontier models before public deployment. The evaluations cover capabilities and security risks, not approvals: CAISI can assess and flag concerns but has no statutory authority to delay or block a release, and the commitments are voluntary. The expansion was catalyzed by the Mythos crisis and a pending executive order that would formalize the process. The practical result is that all five major frontier AI labs are now nominally subject to pre-release government review — a significant structural shift in US AI governance, even if the enforcement mechanism remains entirely dependent on company cooperation. ## 💻 Software Engineering

**"Copy Fail" (CVE-2026-31431) now in CISA KEV — every Linux since 2017 is vulnerable** — Microsoft's Security Response Center disclosed CVE-2026-31431 on May 1, and CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day with a patch deadline of May 15 for federal agencies. The vulnerability is a logic flaw in the Linux kernel's `algif_aead` module (the userspace crypto API's AEAD interface): improper handling of in-place operations during certain copy operations allows any authenticated local user — including container breakouts from cloud VMs — to escalate to root. A 732-byte Python proof-of-concept reliably roots Ubuntu, Amazon Linux, RHEL, and SUSE. The flaw has been present since 2017 and affects millions of Kubernetes clusters. Patches are available in upstream kernel 6.15.1 and in most distro security channels; update or restrict access to `/proc/crypto` as a temporary mitigation on older kernels. [Microsoft Security Blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/05/01/cve-2026-31431-copy-fail-vulnerability-enables-linux-root-privilege-escalation/) | [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/05/cisa-adds-actively-exploited-linux-root.html) | [Bleeping Computer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cisa-says-copy-fail-flaw-now-exploited-to-root-linux-systems/) | [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/04/30/copyfail-linux-lpe-vulnerability-cve-2026-31431/)

**MOVEit Automation authentication bypass (CVE-2026-4670, CVSS 9.8) — patch now** — Progress Software released patches on May 4 for two vulnerabilities in MOVEit Automation discovered privately by Airbus researchers. CVE-2026-4670 (CVSS 9.8) is an authentication bypass in the service backend command port that allows unauthenticated remote attackers to gain administrative control of any MOVEit Automation instance, exposing stored credentials, business data, and connected enterprise networks. A second flaw, CVE-2026-5174 (CVSS 7.7), allows privilege escalation. No exploitation in the wild has been confirmed, but given MOVEit's history as a high-value supply chain attack target (the 2023 Clop ransomware campaign compromised 2,700+ organizations), patching is urgent. Fixed versions: 2025.1.5, 2025.0.9, and 2024.1.8. No workaround exists; a full-installer upgrade is required and will cause downtime. **Git 2.54: geometric maintenance default, new `git history` commands, pluggable object backends** — Released April 20, Git 2.54 makes the geometric repacking strategy the default for `git maintenance run` — meaning incremental maintenance will now automatically organize object packs in exponentially sized tiers rather than running a full `gc`, substantially improving performance on large repos without manual configuration. ## Worth a Deeper Look

**NIST pre-release AI evaluations: the shape of a voluntary oversight regime**

Today's CAISI agreements with Google, Microsoft, and xAI complete a sweep that now covers every major frontier AI lab in the United States. The structure is worth understanding carefully, because it is simultaneously more significant and less powerful than headlines suggest.

What CAISI actually does: it runs models on a suite of non-public evaluations covering cybersecurity capabilities, science, reasoning, math, and safety — the same benchmarks it used in the publicly reported DeepSeek V4 Pro evaluation last week. Companies provide early access to models and CAISI shares findings privately before release. The center has no authority to delay, block, or impose conditions on a deployment. There is no regulation that requires compliance, and any company can withdraw at any time.

What the voluntary regime does accomplish: it creates a structured pre-release window where the government has the option to share findings with companies privately, flag national security concerns through back-channels, and build a body of classified benchmark data about frontier capability trajectories that no public evaluation currently provides. In practice, the companies are choosing to participate because government access is a strategic asset — particularly given the Mythos crisis and the possibility of a formal executive order that would mandate the same review but potentially with more prescriptive conditions. Voluntary compliance before an executive order gives companies more influence over how the eventual formal framework is designed.

The gap that remains: the agreements apply to US-headquartered labs. No equivalent mechanism exists for Chinese frontier models (DeepSeek, Kimi K2.6, GLM-5.1), which are now at cost parity on agentic coding benchmarks. CAISI can evaluate publicly available models post-hoc (as it did with DeepSeek V4 Pro), but that is a categorically different kind of oversight.
