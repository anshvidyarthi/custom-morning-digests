## Worth a deeper look

**GitHub Copilot's June 1 billing shift signals end of flat-rate AI coding assistants**

GitHub's move to token-based billing on June 1, 2026 marks the final nail in the coffin for flat-rate AI coding tool pricing. The shift follows similar moves by Cursor (June 2025) and Windsurf (March 2026), signaling a fundamental industry recognition that LLM inference costs vary too dramatically by feature and model to be sustainably absorbed under fixed subscription pricing.

The current "Premium requests" system—which let users trigger expensive models a certain number of times per month before falling back to cheaper alternatives—was always a bridge mechanism. It isolated high-cost experiences and introduced caps, but still required GitHub to eat the variance between a quick chat question and an hours-long automated coding session. The new AI Credits system makes that variance the user's problem.

GitHub will ship a "preview invoice" experience in early May so users can estimate costs under the new model before the June 1 cutover. That preview will be critical for teams evaluating whether their current usage patterns remain economically viable. For organizations that have grown accustomed to predictable per-seat pricing, the switch to metered consumption will require new budget controls, visibility tooling, and rollout policies.

The broader trend is clear: AI coding tools are converging on the same pricing model as cloud infrastructure—usage-based, with budgets, visibility, and governance as first-class concerns. The days of treating these tools like flat-rate SaaS subscriptions are over, and the operational playbook now looks more like managing AWS spend than managing Jira licenses.

For teams currently piloting or deploying Copilot, the practical action items are: (1) use the May preview invoice to baseline current spend under the new model, (2) set up usage reporting and budget alerts before June 1, and (3) revisit which features (PR summarization, agent mode, code review) deliver measurable productivity gains versus which are nice-to-haves that won't justify their per-token cost.
