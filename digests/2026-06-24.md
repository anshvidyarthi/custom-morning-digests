## Worth a deeper look

**FDA reversal on Hunter syndrome therapy shows rare-disease pathway flexibility** — 
Regenxbio announced Monday that the FDA indicated it would reverse the earlier rejection of Navsunli, the only potential one-time treatment and gene therapy for MPS II (Hunter syndrome); the company expects the Type A meeting to take place in July and to resubmit the BLA rapidly following the meeting in Q3 2026.
 
In February 2026, the FDA issued a Complete Response Letter, citing concerns about study eligibility criteria, the comparability of the natural history external control, and the appropriateness of cerebrospinal fluid heparan sulfate as a surrogate endpoint; separately, in January 2026, the FDA placed a clinical hold on both Navsunli and Regenxbio's MPS I program RGX-111 following a neoplasm case in a patient treated with RGX-111.
 The reversal demonstrates how the FDA is adapting its framework for ultra-rare diseases where conventional controlled trials may not be feasible.

**AI talent reshuffling accelerates as OpenAI and Anthropic compete for DeepMind researchers** — 
Two top DeepMind researchers departed in one week: Noam Shazeer to OpenAI and John Jumper to Anthropic; Jumper, who led the AlphaFold team at Google DeepMind and shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with CEO Demis Hassabis, announced he is leaving after nearly nine years to join Anthropic.
 
Anthropic pre-positioned for a structural biology hire by building VirBench, wet labs, and HHMI/Allen Institute partnerships before announcing Jumper.
 
For Google, the accumulating departures create pressure to address whatever is driving them before the exits become self-reinforcing.
 The dual exodus in a single week signals a competitive moment for talent acquisition among frontier AI labs heading into IPO season.
