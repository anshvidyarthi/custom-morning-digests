## Worth a deeper look

**Ability Neurotech's chronic BCI study marks shift to long-term implant viability**

The May 26, 2026, Dutch regulatory approval for Ability Neurotech represents a critical transition in brain-computer interface development: from acute surgical demonstrations to chronic, real-world deployment. Unlike previous BCI trials that focused on intraoperative testing or short-term proof-of-concept, Ability's approval specifically authorizes chronic implantation—meaning patients will live with the device for extended periods while researchers track long-term performance, biocompatibility, and signal stability.

The technology itself reflects the field's shift toward wireless, minimally invasive architectures. Ability's device uses optical-link transmission rather than traditional wired connections or radiofrequency protocols, which reduces the physical burden on patients and eliminates the need for transcutaneous wires that create infection risk. The electrodes sit on the cortex (epidural placement) rather than penetrating brain tissue, and the implant is thin enough to allow the scalp to close over it. Power and data transfer occur through a magnetically docked wearable device that communicates via laser, enabling high-speed, high-capacity data transmission without batteries inside the body.

For ALS patients, the promise is direct neural communication as motor function declines. The system translates full neural intent into digital action, enabling autonomous text generation, assistive technology interaction, and real-time communication purely from thought. The regulatory pathway through UMC Utrecht and the INTRECOM consortium positions the trial within a leading European BCI research network, which has established protocols for staged testing and long-term safety monitoring.

The approval follows a broader pattern in 2026: BCI companies are moving beyond headline-grabbing first implants and toward the harder work of demonstrating durability, reliability, and safety over months and years. Neuralink has three patients with stable 2,048-electrode recordings beyond 18 months. Synchron has vascular-implanted devices in multiple patients with multi-year follow-up. Precision Neuroscience has demonstrated 16,384-channel recordings in surgery. Ability's chronic trial adds another data point to the emerging evidence that BCIs can maintain stable performance outside the lab.
