## ⚛️ Quantum & Deep Tech

**Superfluid qubits promise 100x error reduction** — 
Researchers at the University of Surrey have proposed a SHOQ (superfluid helium-oscillator qubit) device with predicted error rates roughly 100 times lower than conventional superconducting qubits, achieved by encoding quantum information in superfluid helium to shield against electromagnetic noise and stray electrical charges
. 
Rather than replacing existing hardware, the SHOQ device could potentially be integrated with superconducting quantum technology or serve as quantum memory, storing quantum information while other hardware performs calculations
. [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260930020309.htm)

**Type-I superconductor breaks time symmetry** — 
Physicists at the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research have synthesized ytterbium diantimonide (YbSb2), the first type-I superconductor observed breaking time-reversal symmetry—a property previously exclusive to type-II superconductors—becoming superconducting at around -272°C with spontaneous internal magnetic fields appearing below the transition temperature
. 
The material's ability to break time-reversal symmetry without external magnetic fields may enable it to host gapless Majorana surface modes, making it a candidate for topological quantum materials that protect quantum information from heat and electromagnetic noise
. **D-Wave opens gate-model simulator to beta testers** — 
D-Wave launched a beta program for its gate-model quantum computing simulator accessible via Leap quantum cloud, supporting up to 21 qubits with both ideal and hardware emulation modes and modeling D-Wave's dual-rail superconducting cavity qubit architecture with hardware-level error detection to evaluate mid-circuit detection, test real-time classical feedback control, and establish design rules for quantum machine learning under hardware noise constraints
. Initial participants include financial institutions and research centers. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**NATO publishes quantum technology roadmap** — 
NATO released its official Quantum Technology Roadmap in July 2026 to implement its 2023 Quantum Technology Strategy, outlining Lines of Effort in quantum computing, sensing, and communications with target milestones to accelerate technology adoption and maintain defense interoperability among its 32 member nations, including quantum sensing for positioning/navigation/timing in GNSS-denied environments and the Quantum Zero Beacon Project for secure communications
. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Classiq ships Fault Tolerance Engine** — 
Classiq launched its Fault Tolerance Engine, a new tool integrated into the Classiq SDK for hardware-aware quantum application synthesis
. The SDK update enables developers to design quantum algorithms optimized for error-corrected systems. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Alice & Bob demonstrates DC-biased cat qubit control** — 
Alice & Bob and ENS Lyon demonstrated a DC-biased SQUID architecture for stabilizing cat qubits, replacing traditional microwave pumps with a microvolt-scale DC voltage to eliminate unwanted Kerr nonlinearities and provide scalable control, with the experimental setup showing selective activation of 1-to-1, 2-to-1, and 4-to-1 photon conversion processes that enable deterministic pair-photon extraction for more robust quantum error correction
. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**California allocates $30M for quantum infrastructure** — 
California Governor Gavin Newsom signed legislation and allocated $30 million for quantum and deep-space research to advance the state's technology infrastructure through the Quantum California initiative, supporting university research, startup incubators, and hardware supply chains
. The move positions California to compete with federal quantum programs and international efforts. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**BCG: quantum could prevent billions of tons of emissions** — 
A Boston Consulting Group study estimates that quantum-enabled technologies could eventually prevent 3 billion to 7 billion metric tons of emissions annually if fully deployed—representing nearly a tenth of current global emissions at the midpoint—though only 10% to 15% of potential savings could arrive by 2040 under normal equipment replacement cycles, even if commercially viable quantum solutions emerge around 2035
. 
BCG models quantum computing's 2040 emissions footprint at approximately 90 million metric tons CO2-equivalent, less than 0.2% of projected 50 billion metric tons of global emissions and about 7% of projected data center emissions, with the small footprint attributed to requiring only 230-1,400 quantum computers globally versus more than 11,000 data centers supporting current AI and computing workloads
. [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/02/quantum-computing-could-cut-industrial-emissions-without-an-ai-sized-footprint-bcg-finds/)
