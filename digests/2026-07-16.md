## 📈 Finance & Stock Markets

**June inflation collapse masks renewed energy risks** — 
The Consumer Price Index dropped 0.4% in June, the largest monthly decline since April 2020, driven by a 5.7% plunge in energy costs after a brief Iran ceasefire; headline CPI rose 3.5% year-over-year
. 
Energy prices fell 5.7% in June after rising 3.9% in May, 3.8% in April, and 10.9% in March, contributing the largest share of the monthly decline
. 
Oil prices have since surged more than 15% in July as the ceasefire ended, raising fears that volatile energy prices could reignite inflation and keep the Fed's finger on the rate-hike trigger
. [Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm)

**Wholesale prices surprise to the downside** — 
The Producer Price Index for final demand fell 0.3% in June, beating expectations for no change, after advancing 0.6% in May and 1.1% in April
. 
On an unadjusted basis, PPI increased 5.5% for the 12 months ended in June, while gasoline prices dropped 12% in the month, accounting for nearly two-thirds of the decline in final demand goods
. 
Core PPI rose 0.2% for the month, below the 0.3% forecast, and 4.7% annually, notably lower than the 5.2% forecast
. [Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ppi.nr0.htm)

**Treasury yields edge higher as Middle East tensions return** — 
The 10-year Treasury yield rose to the 4.60% threshold on Thursday, approaching the near two-month high of 4.62%, as renewed U.S.-Iran attacks lifted fuel prices and rekindled inflation concerns, while initial jobless claims fell to an over two-month low and the inflationary outlook drove the FOMC to project a rate hike this year
. 
The 2-year Treasury note yield rose over 2 basis points to 4.158%, while the 30-year Treasury bond yield added 2 basis points to 5.107%
. [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/government-bond-yield)

**TSMC delivers blowout quarter but investors sell the capex news** — 
TSMC announced Q2 revenue of $40.20 billion and net income of $706.56 billion, with EPS soaring 77.4% year-over-year on strong AI chip demand
. 
Investors focused on TSMC's raised capital expenditure forecast of $60 billion to $64 billion for the year, up from prior guidance of $52 billion to $56 billion, overshadowing the earnings beat
. 
The stock fell more than 4% Thursday, hurting other semiconductors and the Nasdaq early, marking the second time in three days solid earnings from a dominant chip firm preceded a sector pullback
. [Taiwan Semiconductor SEC Filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001046179/000104617926000451/a2q26e_withguidancexfinal.htm)

**Chip stocks whipsawed as AI spending skepticism builds** — 
Chip producers fell sharply Thursday on skepticism that AI hyperscalers could slow infrastructure spending, with speculative demand also dented by Korea prohibiting leveraged ETFs and ASML signaling more efficient machines; Micron, AMD, Sandisk, Intel, and Broadcom dropped around 3%
. 
Investors pared exposure to key semiconductor stocks while moving into Big Tech names, with Amazon and Alphabet up around 3%, Microsoft higher by nearly 3%, and Apple gaining 4% to hit a new all-time high on Wednesday
. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/14/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)

**Uber seals $14.8 billion Delivery Hero acquisition** — 
Uber entered a business combination agreement to acquire Delivery Hero for €41.50 per share in cash, representing an equity value of $14.8 billion ($13.7 billion adjusted for Uber's prior stake purchases), extending its platform to 99 markets with combined pro-forma gross bookings of $236 billion in 2025
. 
The acquisition expands Uber's global delivery business through Delivery Hero's portfolio including Foodpanda, Glovo, Talabat, HungerStation and PedidosYa, with the combined business spanning ride-hailing, food delivery and quick commerce
. 
Uber plans to draw on cash on hand and raise new debt, arranging a committed bridge facility of approximately €14 billion, with the deal expected to be accretive to non-GAAP EPS upon closing and high-single-digit percentage accretion by year three
. [Uber Investor Relations](https://investor.uber.com/news-events/news/press-release-details/2026/Uber-Announces-Acquisition-Offer-for-Delivery-Hero/default.aspx)

**PayPal surges 17% on $53 billion Stripe takeover offer** — 
Stripe and private equity firm Advent International submitted a joint bid to acquire PayPal for approximately $53.4 billion, or $60.50 per share, backed by roughly $50 billion in committed bank financing, with the proposal calling for Stripe and Advent to jointly own PayPal with each holding an equal stake
. 
PayPal shares closed up 17.20% to $55.52 on the news, with Polymarket odds at 60% that Stripe will acquire all or part of PayPal, though the offer represents a significant discount to the company's 2021 peak valuation near $300
. 
PayPal has been working with Goldman Sachs and Evercore to review strategic options as the companies pursue the takeover
. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/15/stripe-and-advent-reportedly-offered-to-buy-paypal-for-around-53-4b/)

**Apple wins China AI approval, partners with Alibaba** — 
Apple received government approval to roll out Apple Intelligence in China, with the Cyberspace Administration of China including Apple's generative AI on a list of newly approved providers alongside recent offerings from local companies like Huawei
. 
The approval follows a deal to integrate Alibaba's Qwen AI model into Apple's operating systems, including iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and visionOS
. 
U.S.-listed shares in Alibaba rose 5% in premarket trading on the news, while Apple gained 4% during Wednesday's session to hit a new all-time high
. [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/apple-gets-approval-for-alibaba-powered-iphone-ai-tools-in-china)

**June retail sales edge up as jobless claims fall** — 
Retail sales rose 0.2% in June after increasing a revised 1% in May, while excluding sales at gas stations, retail sales increased a robust 0.7%
. 
Initial unemployment insurance claims totaled 208,000 for the week ending July 11, down 8,000 from the prior week's revised level and below the Dow Jones consensus for 218,000
. 
The Philadelphia Federal Reserve's manufacturing index soared to 41.4 for July, the highest reading in nearly five years
. [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-july-16-2026)

**Bank earnings outperform as Q2 season accelerates** — 
Morgan Stanley beat Wall Street estimates with Q2 earnings of $3.46 per share on $21.35 billion in revenue, topping forecasts of $2.94 per share and $19.64 billion in revenue, up from $2.13 per share on $16.8 billion a year ago
. 
BlackRock shares were up more than 5% after the asset management giant posted second-quarter results that beat the Street, earning $13.91 per share on revenue of $7.08 billion
. 
UnitedHealth surged 7% after beating earnings and raising its full-year guidance
. [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-july-15-2026)

**SpaceX falls below IPO price for first time** — 
SpaceX went public on June 12, 2026, in the largest initial public offering in history on the Nasdaq at $135 per share, briefly pushing its market valuation above $2 trillion; the stock fell below $135 on Wednesday, marking a new all-time low and down nearly 34% from post-listing records, trading near $133.59
. [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-july-15-2026)

The Nasdaq gained 0.62% Wednesday as megacaps like Apple (+4.01%), Alphabet (+3.17%), Meta (+3.07%), Amazon (+3.02%), and Microsoft (+2.78%) climbed, while small caps in the Russell 2000 (+0.39%) caught a fresh bid thanks to cooling inflation data
. Yet 
Thursday brought renewed pressure as the S&P 500 dropped 0.3% and the Nasdaq 100 lost more than 1%, with chip producers falling sharply on skepticism that AI hyperscalers could slow infrastructure spending
. The sector's volatility underscores a market still grappling with whether 2026's massive AI capital commitments—including TSMC's raised capex guidance—signal sustainable growth or an unsustainable buildout that will eventually slow.
