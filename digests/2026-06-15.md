## 🧬 Biotech / Longevity / Neurotech


**Lilly presents first-in-class type II JAK2 inhibitor data for myelofibrosis** — Eli Lilly announced June 13 that its investigational type II JAK2 inhibitor (AJ1-11095) demonstrated an encouraging safety profile and promising clinical activity in patients with myelofibrosis who failed type I JAK2 inhibitor treatment, with 70% achieving spleen volume reduction and symptom improvement.
 
The Phase 1 data showed a 70% SVR35 rate (spleen volume reduction ≥35%) and 70% TSS50 rate (≥50% improvement in symptom burden) at week 12.
 
Lilly added this program following completion of the Ajax Therapeutics acquisition.
 [Lilly press release](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lilly-present-initial-clinical-data-first-class-type-ii-jak2)


**Sanofi's Tzield wins FDA accelerated approval for stage 3 type 1 diabetes** — The FDA granted accelerated approval June 13 to Tzield (teplizumab-mzwv) to delay the decline in endogenous insulin production in children aged 8-17 years recently diagnosed with stage 3 type 1 diabetes.
 
Tzield was first approved in November 2022 to delay onset of stage 3 T1D in adults and children eight years and older diagnosed with stage 2 T1D, then in April 2026 the FDA expanded this indication to include children aged one year and above.
 [Sanofi press release](https://www.sanofi.com/en/media-room/press-releases/2026/2026-06-12-22-09-58-3311349)


**Incyte to acquire Vega Therapeutics for up to $2 billion** — Incyte announced June 8 a definitive agreement to acquire Vega Therapeutics for $1.25 billion upfront plus up to $750 million in milestone payments, adding VGA039, a first-in-class Phase 3 monoclonal antibody for von Willebrand disease with multiple FDA designations.
 
VGA039 is currently in Phase III development for von Willebrand disease (VWD), which affects approximately 135,000 people in the US and stands as the most common inherited bleeding disorder.
 [Incyte SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000879169/000087916926000040/june2026skyline-exhibit991.htm)


**Nexalin reports 77.8% anxiety response rate in Brazil trial** — Nexalin Technology reported June 10 results from an 8-week clinical trial of its 15 mA 77.5 Hz neurostimulation protocol in adults showing a 77.8% anxiety response rate with durable benefits maintained at 8-week follow-up.
 
No serious adverse events, seizures or manic/hypomanic switches were reported, reinforcing the favorable tolerability profile of Nexalin's Deep Intracranial Frequency Stimulation technology.
 [Globe Newswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/10/3309786/0/en/nexalin-technology-reports-compelling-clinical-trial-results-in-brazil-to-support-product-launch-trial-demonstrates-77-8-anxiety-response-rate-and-durable-benefits-across-depressio.html)


**Longevity-LLM foundation model outperforms specialized aging clocks** — A bioRxiv preprint reports Longevity-LLM v0.1, a Qwen3-14B model fine-tuned on DNA methylation, proteomics, clinical biomarker and RNA expression data, achieved a 4.34-year MAE in epigenetic age prediction, surpassing the Horvath multi-tissue clock.
 
The model demonstrates that a single modestly sized LLM can match or replace purpose-built aging clocks across data modalities.
 [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.28.714980v1)

## Worth a deeper look

**Lilly's type II JAK2 inhibitor breakthrough** — The June 13 data from Lilly represents a significant advance for patients with myelofibrosis who have exhausted type I JAK2 inhibitor options. The 70% response rate across both spleen volume and symptom measures is remarkable for a dose-escalation Phase 1 study in a heavily pretreated population. What makes this particularly notable is the reduction in driver mutation variant allele frequency (VAF), which is uncommonly observed with existing type I JAK2 inhibitors. The first-in-class type II mechanism—selectively binding the type II conformation of the JAK2 kinase—appears to offer a genuinely differentiated pharmacology that could provide an option for patients who develop resistance to ruxolitinib and other type I inhibitors. With 78% of patients remaining on study despite dose escalation and no dose-limiting toxicities, the safety profile appears manageable. The data will be presented at the 2026 European Hematology Association meeting in Stockholm.

**Claude Fable 5's brief window and geographic restrictions** — Anthropic's release of Claude Fable 5 on June 9 marks the first time a next-generation frontier model has been made available to the general public, but the circumstances are unusual. The two-week included access window (June 9-22) before switching to usage credits suggests Anthropic is testing capacity and demand under controlled conditions. More significant is the geographic restriction: Fable 5 is available only to US customers following what appears to be a government restriction on Mythos-class capabilities. This represents the first major instance of differential geographic access to a frontier model based on regulatory constraints rather than market strategy. Developers outside the US must continue using Claude Opus 4.8 or alternative models. The restriction raises questions about how AI capabilities will be distributed globally as models approach more sensitive thresholds, and whether geographic fragmentation of AI access will become a permanent feature of the landscape.
