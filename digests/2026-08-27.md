92% of Nvidia's total sales

**Meta agrees to $17.1B settlement in social media addiction case** — 
Meta agreed to pay up to $17.1 billion to 47 states that sued and add child-safety measures to its platforms, resolving claims filed by the states and cutting short a trial that started last week in Oakland, California
. 
Required changes include daily usage limits and "nighttime blocks" for teenagers, enhanced age-assurance measures, and additional tools for parents
. 
States accused Meta of deliberately designing platforms to be addictive and misleading users about the risks
. The settlement, which requires court approval, came one day after Instagram head Adam Mosseri testified in California. [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/meta-settles-social-media-addiction-lawsuit/)

**Dollar General posts 33% EPS jump as discount retailers attract higher-income shoppers** — 
Dollar General reported net income of $550.3 million for Q2 fiscal 2026, up 33.8% from $411.4 million last year, with diluted EPS rising 33.3% to $2.48 versus $1.86 a year ago, including an estimated $0.25 benefit from tariff refunds
. 
Dollar General and Dollar Tree both reported better-than-expected earnings as they have attracted higher-income shoppers, but stocks diverged due to differences in outlooks

Dollar Tree announced better-than-expected revenue of $4.89 billion, up 7% year on year, with GAAP profit of $2.70 per share significantly above consensus

**PCE inflation stays elevated at 3.7% in July, complicating Fed outlook** — 
July's PCE price index registered a seasonally adjusted monthly gain of 0.2%, lifting the year-over-year rate to 3.7%, with each headline figure coming in 0.1 percentage point ahead of the Dow Jones consensus
. 
Core PCE advanced 0.2% monthly and 3.3% annually, landing exactly where analysts expected
. 
Consumer spending, when adjusted for inflation, was flat from the month prior, a sharp slowdown from a 0.4% gain in June
. The sticky readings keep the Fed well above its 2% target and complicate the policy outlook ahead of the September FOMC meeting. [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/july-2026-pce-inflation-data-124921061.html)

**Treasury yields reverse buyback bounce, 30-year hits 5.19%** — 
The yield on US 30 Year Bond rose to 5.19% on August 27, 2026, marking a 0.01 percentage point increase from the previous session
, after 
bond yields climbed Thursday, coming back from the pullback they saw the previous day after Treasury announced an intervention aimed at easing pressure on longer-dated government debt, with 10-year yields moving more than 5 basis points higher to 4.704%
. 
The yield surge is largely a result of mounting investor concerns about persistent US inflation and ballooning government debt, as well as competition from corporate AI-buildout debt that is sapping demand for Treasuries
. 
The national debt pushed past the $40 trillion mark, while record corporate debt issuance tied to AI build-out has contributed to rising term premiums
. [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/30-year-bond-yield)

## Worth a deeper look

**Nvidia's Q3 guide and hyperscaler capex tell the AI infrastructure story** — 
Nvidia said that its supply commitments more than doubled from $119 billion last quarter to $279 billion in the latest period, "primarily related to the procurement of memory," and noted "we continue to make strategic commitments across our supply, infrastructure, and partner ecosystems to capitalize on the substantial growth opportunities ahead of us"
. The guidance increase to $108 billion for Q3 comes as 
Q2 2026 combined hyperscaler capex hit $166.0 billion, up 87% year-over-year and 27% quarter-over-quarter, representing 272% growth over ten quarters
. 
Meta drove the largest single swing, going from $19.8 billion to $31.1 billion in one quarter and narrowing its full-year outlook upward to a range of $130 to $145 billion, while Amazon remained the largest spender in absolute terms at $54.2 billion
. Nvidia's gross margin held at 75%, and the company returned $26 billion to shareholders via buybacks and dividends during the quarter. The results validate the thesis that AI infrastructure spending remains in early innings, with Nvidia's data center dominance intact even as hyperscalers develop in-house chips.
