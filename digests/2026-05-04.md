## 🧬 Biotech / Longevity / Neurotech

**Veppanu (vepdegestrant) wins FDA approval — first PROTAC in clinical use** — Arvinas and Pfizer received FDA clearance on May 1 for Veppanu, the first PROteolysis-TArgeting Chimera (PROTAC) drug to reach patients. It targets ESR1-mutated ER+/HER2− advanced breast cancer — a population that has largely exhausted prior endocrine therapy — and in the pivotal VERITAC-2 trial cut progression risk by 43% vs. standard-of-care fulvestrant (HR 0.57, p=0.0001) with an ORR of 19% vs. 4%. PROTACs recruit the cell's own ubiquitin-proteasome machinery to fully degrade a target protein rather than merely inhibiting its binding site, which matters because resistance mutations can preserve activity even when a drug is bound; degradation eliminates the target entirely. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-vepdegestrant-er-positive-her2-negative-esr1-mutated-advanced-or-metastatic-breast) | [Arvinas](https://ir.arvinas.com/news-releases/news-release-details/arvinas-announces-fda-approval-veppanu-vepdegestrant-treatment) | [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/arvinas-pfizer-fda-approve-veppanu-vepdegestrant-breast-cancer/819108/)

**Life Biosciences' ER-100 becomes first partial epigenetic reprogramming therapy in human trials** — Following FDA clearance of its IND in January 2026, Life Biosciences (co-founded by Harvard's David Sinclair) began recruiting for Phase 1 of ER-100 in Q1. The intravitreal gene therapy uses three Yamanaka transcription factors (OCT4, SOX2, KLF4) to partially reprogram retinal ganglion cells in patients with glaucoma and non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy — chosen because the eye is accessible and self-contained. The trial is not a systemic aging trial, but it is the first time partial epigenetic reprogramming has entered a human body, making it a proof-of-concept milestone for the cellular rejuvenation field regardless of eye-specific results. Preclinical data showed restored visual function and reversal of optic-nerve epigenetic age in aged mice. [Life Biosciences IND announcement](https://www.lifebiosciences.com/life-biosciences-announces-fda-clearance-of-ind-application-for-er-100-in-optic-neuropathies/) | [Lifespan.io](https://lifespan.io/first-human-cellular-reprogramming-trial-cleared-by-the-fda/)

**Synchron's COMMAND study delivers positive 12-month BCI data** — Synchron's COMMAND study — the first FDA-approved IDE trial for a permanently implanted BCI — reported that all six patients with severe bilateral upper-limb paralysis met the primary endpoint (no device-related serious adverse events in 12 months) and successfully used the Stentrode system to perform digital tasks including typing and device control. What distinguishes Synchron from Neuralink is the surgical approach: the Stentrode is deployed endovascularly through the jugular vein and parked in a motor cortex-adjacent sinus — no craniotomy, no dura penetration. Synchron now reports 50+ patients implanted globally. [NeuroNews International](https://neuronewsinternational.com/synchrons-endovascular-bci-achieves-positive-results-in-us-command-study/) | [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/synchrons-brain-computer-interface-safety-trial/728494/)

**Alzheimer's pipeline update: tau-targeting BIIB080 trial enrolls, Leqembi home-use decision due in May** — Two notable Alzheimer's catalysts are converging this month. Biogen's BIIB080, an antisense oligonucleotide targeting tau mRNA, completed Phase 2 enrollment in the CELIA trial with topline readouts expected mid-2026; tau reduction, unlike amyloid clearance, targets the second major pathological driver and could expand the treatment window. Separately, the FDA is expected to rule in May on whether Leqembi (lecanemab) starter doses can be administered at home, a practical step that would reduce the infusion-clinic burden that has limited real-world adoption of the first class of disease-modifying Alzheimer's therapies. ## 🤖 AI

**NIST evaluates DeepSeek V4 Pro: most capable Chinese AI, but ~8 months behind US frontier** — NIST's Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) published its evaluation of DeepSeek V4 Pro on May 3, using 16 non-public benchmarks spanning cyber, software engineering, natural sciences, abstract reasoning, and math. The finding: V4 Pro is the most capable PRC model evaluated to date, performing roughly comparably to GPT-5 — a model released approximately eight months ago — with particular strength in math. ## 💻 Software Engineering

**TypeScript 7.0 Beta drops: Go-native compiler, 10x faster, memory halved** — Microsoft shipped the TypeScript 7.0 Beta on April 21, marking the culmination of Project Corsa — a line-by-line port of the TypeScript compiler from JavaScript to Go. The result: tsc runs roughly 10x faster than TS 6.0, VS Code's language service cold-start for a large project drops from 9.6s to 1.2s, and memory usage is roughly halved. Full incremental, project references, and `--build` mode are all ported and working; the CLI has ~6,000 error tests, with only 74 test cases producing divergent behavior vs TS 6.0. TS 6.0 (JavaScript-based) shipped in March as the final release on the old compiler; TS 7.0 stable is expected mid-2026. ## Worth a Deeper Look

**Veppanu and the PROTAC modality: why this approval matters beyond breast cancer**

The approval of Veppanu is the culmination of roughly a decade of drug development around a mechanism that was purely theoretical when it was first proposed. A PROTAC molecule is bifunctional: one end binds the target protein (in Veppanu's case, the estrogen receptor carrying an ESR1 resistance mutation), the other end binds an E3 ubiquitin ligase (VHL in Veppanu's case). The ternary complex formed causes the cell to tag the target protein for degradation by the proteasome — the same machinery that handles normal protein turnover. Once the target is degraded, the PROTAC is released intact and can catalyze another degradation cycle, making it substoichiometric (a small number of drug molecules can eliminate a large number of target proteins).

This matters because the ESR1 mutation that Veppanu targets is precisely the mutation that arises as resistance to existing estrogen-receptor blocking drugs like fulvestrant. A standard inhibitor can't overcome a resistance mutation that changes the binding site; a PROTAC that routes the entire protein to the proteasome bypasses the mutation entirely. The VERITAC-2 trial enrolled patients who had already progressed on CDK4/6 inhibitors and endocrine therapy — arguably the hardest-to-treat ER+ population — so the efficacy signal is meaningful.

More broadly, the PROTAC approach is target-agnostic in principle. Arvinas has additional programs in prostate cancer (AR degrader), and other companies have PROTACs in development targeting KRAS, BCL-2, BRD4, and tau. Each of these is a protein that has resisted conventional small-molecule drugs for years. The question going forward is whether the bifunctional design (PROTAC molecules tend to be large and require careful linker chemistry for cell penetration) limits oral bioavailability across different targets — Veppanu is an oral once-daily pill, so Arvinas has at least solved this for its specific molecule.
