## ⚛️ Quantum

**Oratomic closes $300M Series A to bypass NISQ and target fault tolerance** — 
Oratomic, a neutral-atom hardware startup, secured $300 million in Series A funding led by ARCH Venture Partners, Spark Capital, and Khosla Ventures, with additional investment from Bezos Expeditions. The company aims to develop fault-tolerant quantum computers using reconfigurable neutral-atom arrays and optical tweezers, bypassing intermediate commercial products to focus on long-term vertical integration and an error-correction architecture. This funding will support engineering infrastructure and recruitment in advanced optics, atomic physics, and classical control hardware.
 The deal is one of the largest single financing rounds in quantum hardware this year. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Diraq and imec fabricate eight-qubit linear array on 300mm CMOS silicon foundry** — 
Diraq and imec successfully fabricated and demonstrated an eight-qubit linear array on a 300 mm CMOS silicon foundry line, marking a significant advancement in silicon-based quantum computing. This achievement, detailed in Nature Communications, showcases the ability to scale quantum dot configurations without sacrificing coherence or control, utilizing industry-standard manufacturing processes. The device exhibits impressive coherence times and a compact readout architecture, paving the way for Diraq's commercial roadmap to develop highly scalable quantum processors.
 The result shows silicon spin qubits can leverage existing semiconductor infrastructure. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Quantinuum's 98-qubit Helios processor published in Nature with industry-leading fidelity** — 
Quantinuum's Helios is a 98-qubit trapped-ion quantum processor based on the quantum charge-coupled device (QCCD) architecture. Helios features 137Ba+ hyperfine qubits, all-to-all connectivity enabled by a rotatable ion storage ring connecting two quantum operation regions by a junction, speed improvements from parallelized operations and a new software stack with real-time compilation of dynamic programs. Averaged over all operational zones in the system, the team achieved average infidelities of 2.5(1) × 10−5 for single-qubit gates, 7.9(2) × 10−4 for two-qubit gates and 3.3(5) × 10−4 for state preparation and measurement.
 The Nature publication validates Sandia's earlier benchmarking work from July 3. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10676-4)

**SEEQC files S-1 for Nasdaq IPO alongside ongoing Allegro SPAC merger** — 
SEEQC, a quantum hardware company, has filed an S-1 registration for a Nasdaq IPO under the ticker SEQC, running parallel to its merger with Allegro Merger Corp.
 The dual-track approach gives the superconducting quantum computing firm flexibility in its path to public markets. SEEQC focuses on energy-efficient quantum systems using single-flux-quantum logic. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Pasqal and MegazoneCloud sign MoU for neutral-atom deployment in South Korea** — 
Pasqal and MegazoneCloud have partnered to bring neutral-atom quantum hardware to South Korea, integrating Pasqal's technology into MegazoneCloud's managed cloud services. This collaboration aims to provide South Korean companies with access to quantum computing for various industrial applications, including financial services, logistics, biotechnology, and manufacturing, with future plans for on-premises Quantum Processing Unit installations.
 The partnership extends Pasqal's Asia-Pacific footprint beyond its existing Singapore presence. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**QUDORA and QAI integrate trapped-ion systems into South Korean AI data centers** — 
QUDORA and QAI have partnered to integrate QUDORA's trapped-ion quantum computers into QAI's AI data centers in South Korea, aiming to create a co-processing testbed.
 The deployment represents a bet on hybrid quantum-classical workflows where quantum processors handle specific subroutines within broader AI pipelines. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**QAI Ventures launches inaugural Singapore quantum accelerator with SGD 300K per startup** — 
QAI Ventures launched its first Singapore Quantum Accelerator cohort, partnering with Enterprise Singapore to boost early-stage quantum and advanced computing ventures in the APAC region. The program offers SGD 300,000 to each of the four selected startups, along with workspace, market-entry coaching, and access to quantum hardware from IonQ, QuEra, and Fujitsu. The five-month curriculum runs from July to October 2026, culminating in a Demo Day for investors.
 [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Shanghai establishes dual quantum hubs targeting 100+ enterprises by 2029** — 
Shanghai has established two new quantum technology hubs: "Zhangjiang Quantum Bay" in Pudong and the "Shanghai Quantum Computing Future Industry Incubation Zone" in Xuhui. This initiative, part of China's 15th Five-Year Plan, aims to attract over 100 quantum enterprises within three years, focusing on neutral atom quantum computing and integrating with AI resources to develop commercial applications and secure a leading position in the global quantum technology market.
 The move signals escalating state-level quantum investment across China. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Illinois launches $3M X-Labs Fast Fund to magnetize federal quantum teams** — 
Illinois has launched the $3 million X-Labs Fast Fund to attract federal quantum engineering teams, offering immediate capital to those awarded under Phase 0 of the NSF X-Labs competition.
 The fund provides bridge financing to help academic quantum projects transition toward commercialization before larger federal awards arrive. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Rice and Max Planck establish Q-RaMP to accelerate quantum materials synthesis** — 
Rice University and the Max Planck Society have established Q-RaMP, a transnational research initiative to accelerate the discovery and synthesis of quantum materials. This partnership aims to bridge the gap between theoretical models and stable physical quantum crystals by combining the strengths of both institutions in solid-state physics, materials science, and computational engineering. Q-RaMP will focus on isolating and synthesizing complex quantum structures, fostering collaboration through joint faculty appointments, graduate exchange programs, and access to advanced research facilities.
 The collaboration addresses a bottleneck in scaling quantum hardware: reliable materials. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Ohio State secures $4M NSF Phase II award to lead distributed quantum sensing testbed** — 
The Ohio State University secured a $4 million NSF Phase II award to lead the Distributed-Entanglement Quantum Sensing of Chemical Properties (DQS-CP) project. This initiative aims to develop a multi-component quantum sensing platform for precise atomic and molecular interaction capture, potentially revolutionizing industrial and biomedical applications. The project involves a national consortium of universities and industry partners, focusing on commercialization and workforce development in quantum infrastructure.
 [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**CSIRO deploys field-ready quantum light sources for secure satellite time transfer** — 
CSIRO has developed and deployed two field-ready Quantum Light Sources to provide secure timing synchronization for the Australian Defence Force.
 Quantum-based timing sources offer resistance to GPS spoofing and jamming, a growing concern for defense and critical infrastructure operators. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

**Quantum Rings' Open Quantum platform now available on qBraid** — 
Quantum Rings announced a partnership with qBraid to make their Open Quantum platform available within the qBraid Lab. The development provides developers and researchers free, subsidized access to QPUs from IonQ, Rigetti, IQM, and AQT. With Open Quantum, users have a unified access point to run programs and try out different quantum architectures by just changing a single line of code.
 The integration lowers the switching cost for researchers experimenting across hardware vendors. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)

## Worth a deeper look

**White House executive orders on quantum: innovation and post-quantum cryptography mandates** — On June 22, the White House issued two executive orders simultaneously targeting quantum opportunity and quantum threat. 
America stands at the cusp of a quantum revolution. Quantum information science and technology (QIST) will provide transformational capabilities that will drive American innovation, power economic growth, generate high-paying jobs, and bolster national security. In 2018, the National Quantum Initiative Act was signed into law, doubling Federal investment in QIST research and development. Today, as other nations move quickly to challenge American leadership, the United States must take a cohesive, whole-of-government approach to accelerate deployment and commercialization of quantum computing, sensing, and networking.
 The first order establishes the Quantum Computer for Application Development and Discovery Science (QC-ADDS) effort and directs federal agencies to develop five-year plans for quantum sensing and networking deployment. 
The advent of large-scale quantum computers, particularly in the hands of adversaries, will pose a significant threat to widely used cryptographic security systems. Ongoing cyber activity against our Nation also presents the risk of adversaries collecting United States information now, and decrypting it later once large-scale quantum computers are operational. In light of these threats, the United States must take steps to strengthen cryptographic protections for the Nation's sensitive data, critical infrastructure, and digital economy. It is the policy of the United States to safeguard national security and maintain technological leadership by responsibly and effectively executing the transition of Federal information systems to National Institute of Standards and Technology-approved Federal Information Processing Standards for Post-Quantum Cryptography, and to assist critical infrastructure owners and operators with their transitions.
 The cryptography order sets a 2031 deadline for full PQC migration across federal systems and mandates contractors meet NIST FIPS standards by end of 2030, dramatically compressing the timeline compared to earlier guidance. [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/ushering-in-the-next-frontier-of-quantum-innovation/)
