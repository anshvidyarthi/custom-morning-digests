## ⚛️ Quantum & Deep Tech


**George Mason, TreQ to deploy $7.7M open-architecture quantum system in Virginia** — George Mason University is partnering with Oxford-based TreQ to install a $7.7 million open-architecture quantum computer at its Northern Virginia campus, supported by funding from the Virginia Innovation Partnership Corporation. The system, featuring TreQ's Open Architecture Quantum (OAQ) framework, will be the first U.S. deployment of its kind and is expected to be operational by late summer 2027.
 The modular platform allows swappable components from multiple vendors, breaking single-vendor lock-in and enabling rapid hardware prototyping for the National Capital Region's defense and commercial quantum ecosystems. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)


**Quobly partners with TNO and OrangeQS to scale silicon spin qubit manufacturing** — French silicon quantum computing developer Quobly has signed agreements with TNO and Orange Quantum Systems in the Netherlands to advance silicon spin qubit manufacturing. These partnerships, formed during a Franco-Dutch state visit, focus on device characterization, metrology, and high-throughput screening of qubits on 300mm FD-SOI CMOS wafers, integrating with Quobly's existing manufacturing pipeline and aiming for cloud-based HPC deployment by late 2026.
 
Led by CEO Maud Vinet, Quobly's roadmap targets initial cloud-based HPC deployment of its Alloy product family by late 2026, scaling toward one million physical qubits by 2032.
 [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)


**SEEQC signs Taiwan MoU to build quantum cryo-chip supply chain** — SEEQC has signed an MoU with Taiwan's Quantum Industry Technology Promotion Office to create a cross-border quantum technology and supply-chain partnership. This agreement, signed at SEMICON Taiwan 2026, focuses on leveraging Taiwan's semiconductor infrastructure for the development, testing, and commercialization of quantum computing components, including cryo-CMOS and SFQ manufacturing, and advanced packaging for superconducting QPUs. This collaboration aims to integrate SEEQC's IP with Taiwan's foundry capabilities to establish a robust supply chain for commercial multi-qubit systems.
 The MoU formalizes partnerships established in January 2026 across room-temperature electronics and superconducting chip manufacturing. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)


**MIT develops flexible, transparent silicon-photonics chips on 300mm wafers** — MIT scientists have figured out a scalable way to make silicon-photonics chips flexible and transparent, opening a route to advanced microchips for applications such as discreet health monitors that conform to the body or transparent augmented-reality displays. The MIT researchers, working with engineers at NY Creates at the Albany NanoTech Complex, created a fabrication process that uses standard semiconductor manufacturing techniques to generate flexible and transparent silicon-photonics chips on large-scale wafers.
 
The researchers bent a single chip thousands of times around cylinders with various diameters—down to the width of a small screw—with no drop in performance.
 The work, published in *Optica*, could enable curved AR displays for pilots and wearable optical sensors. [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2026/fabrication-platform-could-enable-flexible-transparent-next-generation-photonic-chips-0903)


**Quantinuum and Aramco sign MoU for energy-sector quantum R&D** — Quantinuum and Aramco have signed a non-binding MoU to explore quantum computing applications in the energy sector. This collaboration involves Aramco gaining access to Quantinuum's trapped-ion hardware and software stack to research fault-tolerant quantum computing for complex simulations in energy production and to integrate quantum solutions into their digital transformation.
 The agreement focuses on simulation-intensive workflows including reservoir modeling and molecular dynamics for catalysis, positioning quantum hardware for industrial use cases ahead of full fault tolerance. [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/news/)


**AI-guided discovery identifies two new kagome superconductors** — Scientists have combined machine learning with quantum physics to discover two new superconductors and create a much faster way to search for many more. The technique could bring researchers significantly closer to the long-sought goal of a room-temperature superconductor.
 
The discovery includes kagome superconductors YRu3B2 and LuRu3B2, published in *Physical Review Research*.
 The SuperC consortium used machine learning to narrow candidate materials from over 100,000 compounds to a testable shortlist, achieving experimental validation within months rather than years. [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260701205006.htm)


**University of Houston breaks 30-year ambient-pressure superconductivity record** — A major milestone came in 1987 when researchers discovered that a material known as YBCO could become superconducting at minus 180°C, or 93K. In 1993, scientists discovered Hg1223 that reached superconductivity at minus 140°C, or 133K. That material held the ambient-pressure record for more than 30 years. The new University of Houston achievement pushes the record 18 degrees C higher to 151K.
 
The breakthrough relied on a process known as pressure quenching.
 The work, published in 2026, marks the first ambient-pressure superconductivity advance above the Hg1223 barrier since 1993. [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/05/260527023220.htm)


**Chalmers researchers boost superconductivity via nanofaceted substrates** — Researchers at Chalmers University of Technology in Sweden have developed a new material design that addresses a major obstacle in the field: enabling superconductivity to operate at higher temperatures while also withstanding strong magnetic fields. This shows that very small changes at the nanoscale can have decisive effects and may even unlock the full potential of superconductivity in future electronics.
 
The breakthrough came when the Chalmers team introduced nanoscale adjustments to the substrate surface. Because the atoms in the substrate are arranged in a specific pattern, they can 'guide' how the atoms in the superconducting layer settle.
 The technique, published in *Nature Communications*, uses ultrathin YBa2Cu3O7−δ films on nanofaceted MgO substrates. [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-03-superconductor-advancement-ultra-energy-efficient.html)


**China's EAST tokamak verifies density-free plasma operating regime** — Researchers operating China's Experimental Advanced Superconducting Tokamak (EAST) report the first experimental verification of a theorised density-free plasma operating regime, achieving stable electron densities approximately 1.3–1.65 times the Greenwald limit.
 The January 2026 result, if independently replicated, could reduce plasma disruption risk in future fusion reactors by operating stably above conventional density limits—a key obstacle for ITER and commercial tokamak designs.
