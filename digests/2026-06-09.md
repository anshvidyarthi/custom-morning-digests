## Worth a deeper look


**Microbiome as reversible aging clock** — A February 2026 bioRxiv preprint demonstrates that microbiome depletion through antibiotic treatment in aged mice significantly increased adult neural progenitor cell proliferation in the hippocampus and led to higher numbers of newly generated neurons. The research team identified eotaxin-1 as the key mediator: antibiotic treatment lowered circulating eotaxin-1 levels, and importantly, restoring eotaxin-1 during the antibiotic regimen abolished the rejuvenating effects on both blood vessel density and adult neurogenesis. The findings strengthen the endothelial barrier in the aging brain and enhance vascular density, suggesting the gut microbiome may function as a modifiable driver of brain aging rather than a passive bystander. The implications extend beyond mice—elevated eotaxin-1 has been linked to cognitive decline in humans, making this pathway a potential intervention target.



**OpenCode disrupts AI coding landscape** — OpenCode's ascent to the #1 open-source coding agent slot in June 2026, with 160K+ GitHub stars and 7.5M monthly active users, represents a fundamental shift in the AI coding tools market. Unlike proprietary alternatives locked to single providers, OpenCode offers model-agnostic access to 75+ LLM providers, LSP integration, and air-gapped deployment under an MIT license. The core differentiator is breadth combined with a plan-first execution model where developers approve actions before the agent executes—better for exploratory sessions where understanding intent matters more than speed. The tool's success challenges the assumption that best-in-class developer tools must come from well-funded companies; instead, OpenCode demonstrates that open architecture and model flexibility can overcome ecosystem advantages held by incumbents like GitHub Copilot and Cursor.
