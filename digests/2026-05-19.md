**First-in-human CRISPR gene therapy trial for HIV shows mixed results** — 

The first in-human trial of a CRISPR Cas system delivered by adeno-associated virus 9 gene therapy to treat HIV was able to target only the intended DNA and cleared the blood within 6 months.

## Worth a deeper look

**EU AI Act omnibus agreement: what the December 2027 deadline extension means for high-risk systems**

The May 7, 2026 provisional agreement on the Digital Omnibus amendments represents the first major recalibration of the EU AI Act's timeline since its June 2024 adoption. The most significant change is a 16-month deferral for high-risk AI systems classified under Annex III (use-based systems), pushing compliance from August 2, 2026 to December 2, 2027. Product-regulated systems under Annex I — including medical devices, radio equipment, and lifts — receive an additional year, with obligations now due August 2, 2028 instead of August 2027.

This isn't a sign of regulatory retreat but pragmatic acknowledgment that the infrastructure required for compliance — third-party conformity assessments, harmonized standards, national regulatory sandboxes — is not yet operational at scale. The delayed timeframes reflect challenges with operationalizing several provisions, particularly for high-risk systems requiring testing, documentation, and third-party assessment. Member States were supposed to establish at least one national regulatory sandbox by August 2, 2026; that obligation is now deferred a full year to August 2027.

The omnibus also introduces two new prohibited AI practices: the use of AI systems to generate or manipulate non-consensual intimate material and child sexual abuse material (CSAM). These prohibitions take effect December 2, 2026, well ahead of the broader high-risk system timeline, signaling that certain harms are politically non-negotiable even as enforcement timelines for other provisions shift.

For organizations deploying AI in the EU, the practical takeaway is nuanced. The deferral buys time, but it does not eliminate compliance obligations. Prohibited practices remain prohibited, transparency obligations for general-purpose AI models were already in force as of August 2025, and the risk-based framework itself is unchanged. What has shifted is the enforcement timeline for the most operationally complex category — high-risk systems that require conformity assessments, quality management systems, and ongoing monitoring.

The amendments also clarify the interplay between the AI Act and EU product safety laws, particularly the Machinery Regulation, to avoid duplication between sectoral and AI rules. AI systems used solely for user assistance, performance optimization, efficiency or automation, and convenience or quality control will no longer qualify as "safety components" (and thus high-risk AI) unless a failure or malfunction could endanger health or safety. This narrows the scope of what falls under the most stringent requirements.

For U.S. companies selling into the EU, the deferral does not change the fundamental calculus: if your AI system will be used in the EU and falls into a high-risk category, you will need to comply. The December 2027 deadline is firm, and organizations that wait until late 2027 to begin compliance work will face the same crunch that prompted the deferral in the first place. The lead time for technical documentation, risk management systems, and third-party assessments is measured in quarters, not weeks.
