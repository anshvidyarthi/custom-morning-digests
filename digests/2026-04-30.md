## 🧬 Biotech / Longevity / Neurotech

**Life Biosciences initiates first epigenetic reprogramming human trial** — The David Sinclair co-founded company received FDA IND clearance in January and has now started a Phase 1 study enrolling patients with open-angle glaucoma and non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy. The therapy (ER-100) uses three Yamanaka factors (OCT4, SOX2, KLF4) to partially reverse epigenetic age in retinal cells without altering DNA. This is a landmark first step for the broader partial reprogramming field. [longevity.technology](https://longevity.technology/news/fda-clears-first-human-trial-of-epigenetic-reprogramming-therapy/)

**FDA approves first gene therapy for genetic hearing loss** — On April 23, the FDA approved Otarmeni (lunsotogene parvec-cwha), an AAV-based gene therapy for OTOF-related hearing loss, a congenital condition caused by mutations in the otoferlin gene. This is the first gene therapy approval for hearing in the US and marks a shift from hearing aids to curative approaches for a subset of deaf patients. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/novel-drug-approvals-fda/novel-drug-approvals-2026)

**FDA approves oral GLP-1 for obesity** — Foundayo (orforglipron, Eli Lilly) received approval April 1 as the first oral small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist for obesity. Unlike semaglutide or tirzepatide, it requires no injection and no fasting window, potentially broadening access significantly. Clinical data showed ~15% body weight loss over 36 weeks. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/novel-drug-approvals-fda/novel-drug-approvals-2026)

**Neuralink shows patients controlling robotic arms with thought** — Interim results from the company's expanding trial (45 participants, three continents) show 85% of spinal cord injury participants completing pick-and-place tasks within 150% of non-injured baselines. The locked-in syndrome cohort hit >80% open-vocabulary word recognition accuracy, surpassing historical EEG benchmarks. High-volume automated surgical production is planned for later this year. [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/elon-musks-neuralink-kickstart-high-volume-production-brain-computer-interface-devices)

**Epia Neuro launches stroke-focused BCI** — The San Francisco startup emerged from stealth in early April with an implantable read/write BCI paired with a motorized rehabilitation glove. The implant sits within the skull without piercing the dura, enabling a sub-one-hour surgical procedure. First-in-human demonstrations are slated for later in 2026 at Lenox Hill Hospital's neurosurgery department. [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/epia-neuro-launches-bci-device-help-stroke-patients-rework-their-brain-signals)

**AstraZeneca's Saphnelo clears FDA for self-administration** — The systemic lupus erythematosus drug earned clearance for a once-weekly autoinjector format, shifting infusions to home use and potentially improving adherence among the roughly 16,000 US patients currently on the drug. [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fierce-pharma-regulatory-tracker-2026)

## 🤖 AI

**Microsoft and OpenAI end exclusive cloud partnership** — In a deal restructured April 27, Microsoft dropped its exclusive right to resell OpenAI models and removed the AGI clause that had restricted OpenAI's commercial freedom. OpenAI can now sell products via AWS, Google Cloud, or any other provider. Microsoft retains a non-exclusive IP license through 2032 and stops paying a revenue share to OpenAI (OpenAI will continue paying one to Microsoft). This unblocks OpenAI's $50B Amazon deal that had been legally ambiguous. [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-27/microsoft-to-stop-sharing-revenue-with-main-ai-partner-openai) | [Microsoft Blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/04/27/the-next-phase-of-the-microsoft-openai-partnership/)

**GitHub Copilot moves to token-based billing** — Starting June 1, GitHub replaces its flat-rate premium-request model with token consumption billing across all Copilot tiers. Existing plan prices hold ($10/mo Pro, $39/mo Pro+), but usage beyond included credit allotments is metered at API rates. New Pro/Pro+ sign-ups are paused since April 20; Opus-class models are no longer available on Pro plans. Code review will also begin consuming GitHub Actions minutes. [GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-copilot-is-moving-to-usage-based-billing/) | [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/28/microsofts_github_shifts_to_metered/)

**Google Genie 3 available to AI Ultra subscribers** — DeepMind's interactive world model now generates navigable 3D environments at 720p/24fps from text or image prompts, running in real time. Unlike Genie 2's 10–20 second clips, Genie 3 sustains consistent worlds for several minutes and supports mid-session text commands to alter the environment. Available today to US AI Ultra users. [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/)

**Human scientists still outperform best AI agents on complex research tasks** — A Nature-published study found that top AI agents (including frontier models) were significantly outpaced by human scientists on tasks requiring multi-step experimental design, domain judgment, and novel hypothesis generation. The gap was widest on open-ended tasks where no ground-truth scaffold existed. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01199-z)

**Google invests up to $40B in Anthropic** — The deal, reported this week, makes Google the largest single investor in Anthropic and deepens Anthropic's dependence on Google Cloud infrastructure. The commitment includes both cash and compute credits and comes as Anthropic's annualized revenue reportedly crossed $3B. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/24/google-to-invest-up-to-40b-in-anthropic-in-cash-and-compute/)

**Frontier models cross 50% on Humanity's Last Exam** — The Stanford AI Index 2026 highlights that Claude Opus 4.6 and Gemini 3.1 Pro have crossed 50% accuracy on HLE, the hardest publicly available benchmark. A year ago no model exceeded 10%. The index also notes AI now appears in 6–9% of all natural science publications, up nearly 30-fold since 2010. [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/state-of-ai-index-2026)

**China blocks Meta's acquisition of AI startup Manus** — Regulators in China rejected the deal, which would have given Meta access to Manus's autonomous agent platform. The decision adds to an increasingly fragmented global AI acquisition landscape where geopolitical review has become a standard deal risk. [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/news)

## 💻 Software Engineering

**OpenAI multi-cloud distribution unlocked for enterprise** — Beyond the AI policy implications, the engineering consequence of the Microsoft–OpenAI restructure is that enterprise teams locked into Azure for GPT model access can now procure via AWS Bedrock or Google Vertex AI. Expect multi-cloud AI deployment patterns to accelerate as a real architectural concern rather than a formality. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/27/openai-microsoft-partnership-revenue-cap.html)

**Apple mandates iOS/tvOS/watchOS 26 SDK for App Store submissions** — Starting April 28, all new app and game uploads to App Store Connect must be built with the iOS 26, tvOS 26, visionOS 26, and watchOS 26 SDKs. Developers who haven't migrated CI pipelines to Xcode for the new SDK need to act before submitting any new builds. [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/)

**pgbackrest no longer maintained** — The widely-used PostgreSQL backup tool has been abandoned by its primary maintainer. This affects a large share of self-hosted Postgres deployments. The Hacker News thread surfaced active forks and alternatives including WAL-G and pgmoneta, but no clear canonical successor has emerged. [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/news)

**Quarkdown: programmable Markdown for technical documents** — A trending open-source project extending Markdown with a scripting layer — variables, loops, functions, conditional blocks — that compiles to HTML, PDF, or slide decks. Positioned as a lightweight LaTeX alternative for technical writing without full pandoc pipelines. [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/news)

**ASML as the global chip chokepoint** — A detailed piece examining how ASML's monopoly on EUV lithography machines gives it (and by extension Netherlands and US export controls) outsized leverage over the global semiconductor supply chain. Timely given continued US-China chip restrictions and ASML's role in constraining Chinese fab advancement. [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/news)

## Worth a Deeper Look

**The Microsoft–OpenAI restructure: what it actually changes**

Monday's announcement was framed as a partnership "maturation," but the operational consequences are substantial. Until now, OpenAI's exclusivity with Microsoft meant enterprise customers needed Azure to access GPT models via API, and any deal OpenAI signed with a competing cloud — including Amazon's $50B commitment — operated in a legal grey zone. That constraint is now gone. OpenAI can pursue hyperscaler-agnostic distribution, which matters enormously for enterprise sales cycles where cloud vendor lock-in is often the deciding factor in AI procurement. On the financial side, Microsoft surrendering its revenue-share claim on OpenAI resales likely reflects confidence that Azure AI services — which now include a wide roster of non-OpenAI models — can stand independently. The removal of the AGI clause is arguably the most philosophically significant change: Microsoft's prior contractual right to halt commercialization if OpenAI declared AGI achieved is eliminated, removing a governance lever with no parallel elsewhere in the industry. For developers and architects, the practical implication is that model routing across clouds becomes a real design decision rather than a formality — and that API abstraction layers (LiteLLM, Portkey, etc.) are well-positioned to benefit.
